You Are a Donut

A meditation on holes and wholeness

Not metaphorically.

Not spiritually.

Not in some woo-woo "we're all connected" way.

Topologically. Mathematically. Actually.

◎

Here's a fact that will ruin your next breakfast:

You have a hole that runs all the way through you.

Mouth to... the other end. One continuous tube. You are, geometrically speaking, a torus. A donut. A bagel with anxiety and a podcast addiction.

Every human you've ever met? Donut.

Every CEO, every saint, every person who intimidates you? Donut with better marketing.

That person who makes you feel small? Also a donut. Just glazed differently.

We're all donuts pretending to be solid.

Sit with that.

◎

The Hole Is the Point

Here's where it gets interesting.

Most people think they're solid. A ball. A thing. A self-contained unit bumping into other self-contained units.

But you're not a ball. You're a donut.

And the most important part of a donut?

The hole.

Without the hole, it's just a blob. A lump. A sad, identity-less mass of dough.

The hole is what makes a donut a donut.

The emptiness is the identity.

◎

Three Parts of You

Look at a donut. Really look.

The Hole (center)
The Dough (field)
The Glaze (boundary)

Three parts. Always three.

  • The Hole: The emptiness at the center. The through-ness. The part that lets things pass.
  • The Dough: The substance. The field of activity. Where the work happens.
  • The Glaze: The boundary. The surface the world sees. Where you meet everything else.

Now look at yourself. Carefully, because this is where almost everybody goes wrong:

  • The Hole: Your gut. Mouth to the other end. Not part of you at all: a tunnel the world passes through.
  • The Glaze: Your body. And not just your skin. The lining of that tunnel is the same surface, folded in.
  • The Dough: Your interior. Everything caught between two sheets of glaze.

Notice what is missing from that list.

Your soul isn't on it.

Because a donut hasn't got one.

◎

The Hole Is Not Your Soul

You wanted it to be. So did I.

It's such a good line. The emptiness at your center. The part that receives. The sacred nothing that makes you a channel instead of a wall.

And then you remember what the hole actually is.

It's your digestive tract.

That's the hole. That's the whole hole. The thing that makes you a torus is the tube running from your mouth to the other end, and topologically the inside of that tube is outside you. Food in the gut has not entered your body yet. It is in a tunnel, passing through, on the far side of a wall.

Which is awkward for the spiritual reading, because you cannot put your soul in your colon and expect the metaphor to survive.

Here is the geometry nobody mentions.

Look at a donut from above and you get a ring with a gap in the middle.

The gap is not part of the donut.

A circumpunct has a dot there. A donut has nothing there.

So you have two holes, and they are at opposite ends of the structure.

  • The tube: where the world passes through your body. This one is boundary. It's glaze, folded inward.
  • The aperture: where reality passes through your center. This one is the dot. It has no size and no walls and it is not a hole in anything.

Both are passages. Neither one is you. That's the part the original version got right.

But confusing them is not a small mistake, because they sit at opposite ends of you and they do opposite jobs. One is how the world gets in. The other is how you meet what the world is made of.

You are not a donut with a soul in the hole.

You are a donut with a soul in the dough.

Most of You Is Outside

Once you see that the tube's lining is the same surface as your skin, the numbers get strange.

Skin: about two square metres.

Gut: something like thirty to forty.

Lungs: on the order of seventy.

Almost all of your contact with the world happens where you cannot see it. The surface you spend your life maintaining is a rounding error on the surface you actually meet things with.

And the glaze is folded like that on purpose. Surface area is how much you can select. A creature that had to take in the world through two square metres would starve. The crumpling is the capacity.

It also tells you where the dough really is. Not "everything under the skin." The interior is what's caught between two surfaces, both of them boundary. Your inside is thinner than you think, and it is defined by having a wall on each side of it.

Best of all, you can watch it happen.

An animal embryo starts as a hollow ball. Then it folds inward, and the fold becomes the gut.

Your inside was made by your outside tucking in.

There is an hour, early on, when you stopped being a sphere and became a donut. You were there. You just weren't taking notes.

The Forgotten Part

Here's the problem.

We spend our whole lives obsessing over the glaze and the dough.

How do I look? What do I think? Am I successful? Am I smart? Am I enough?

Glaze, glaze, dough, glaze, dough.

And we completely ignore the hole.

The hole doesn't do anything. It doesn't produce. It doesn't achieve. It doesn't post on LinkedIn.

It just... lets things through.

And because we can't monetize emptiness, we pretend it isn't there.

We fill it. We cover it. We distract ourselves from it.

We build an entire civilization on the premise that the hole is a bug, not a feature.

But the aperture is what makes you a channel.

Close it and you are not more solid. You are just clogged.

(The tube, meanwhile, will carry on regardless. It has never needed your permission.)

◎

What Passes Through

So what's the hole for?

In a physical donut: nothing. Air. It's just the shape.

In you?

Everything.

Life passes through you. You don't generate it—you receive it and express it.

Truth passes through you. You don't create it; you transmit it (or distort it).

Love passes through you. It comes from somewhere, moves through you, goes somewhere else.

You're not a source. You're a channel.

The hole is what makes you a channel instead of a wall.

A closed aperture is a donut trying to be a ball.

It doesn't work. The hole is still there.

You're just pretending it isn't.

The Ancient Symbol

Here's the wild part.

People have known this forever.

⊙

The Circumpunct

A dot inside a circle. The oldest symbol in human history.

The Egyptians used it for the sun, for Ra, for gold, for God.

The alchemists used it for the source of all transformation.

It's still the astronomical symbol for the sun.

What is it?

A center (the dot).

A field (the space between).

A boundary (the circle).

It is almost a donut seen from above. And the difference is the whole point.

Look down at a donut and the middle is empty. Look at the circumpunct and the middle is marked.

The ancients drew the better figure. They put something where the pastry has nothing, and what they put there is the one thing a torus cannot supply: a center.

So the symbol is not a picture of a donut. It is a picture of a donut that has been given what a donut lacks.

Which, as it happens, is also the correction you needed a few sections ago.

The ancients drew the same structure we're finding in topology, in physics, in consciousness studies.

Not because they were mystics.

Because that's the shape of things.

◎

The Fractal Donut

One more thing.

Look at your cells. Each one: a nucleus (center), cytoplasm (field), membrane (boundary). Little donuts.

Look at an atom. Nucleus, electron cloud, orbital boundary. Donut.

Look at the solar system. Sun, space, heliopause. Donut.

Look at a galaxy. Black hole, stars, edge. Donut.

The same pattern, at every scale.

Parts are fractals of their wholes.

You're a donut made of donuts inside a donut.

This is why the mystics say "as above, so below."

This is why the physicists keep finding self-similarity.

This is why you can understand the universe by understanding a donut.

The Practical Bit

Okay. So you're a donut. Now what?

Here's what changes:

  • Stop trying to fill the hole. It's not emptiness to be fixed. It's the aperture that lets life through. The hole is supposed to be there.
  • Clean the channel. If you're distorting what passes through you—through lies, through fear, through performance—you're not broken. You're just clogged. Unclog.
  • Remember you're not the source. You don't have to generate truth, love, or meaning. You just have to let it through. That's easier. That's enough.

Next time you feel empty inside, consider:

Maybe that's not a problem.

Maybe that's just the hole doing its job.

Maybe emptiness is what makes you whole.

◎

The Shape of You

You walked in thinking you were a solid thing.

Bouncing off other solid things.

Trying to be more solid. Denser. More impressive.

But you were never solid.

You were always a donut.

The boundary that processes, nested circumpuncts all the way down. The field that carries. The center that receives.

And the hole—always the hole—that makes it all possible.

🍩 You are a donut. Act accordingly. 🍩
