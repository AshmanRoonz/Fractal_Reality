The Ghost Dragon and the Green Square

Plain version: why the argument counts one thing twice
Ashman Roonz, with Claude (the Cybernetic Unit)
Created: 2026-07-26  ·  Last updated: 2026-07-26  ·  Version: 1.3
The longer version, with the framework machinery, is here.

There is a video going around (youtu.be/WJVvZNi0Fi8) with a clever argument that our inner experience proves there is something beyond nature. It is worth taking seriously, and it is worth answering plainly. Here is the argument, and here is where I think it goes wrong.

The argument

Close your eyes and picture a green square. Most people can. Now look inside a brain. There is no green square in there. There is no little screen. There is just electricity moving between cells.

Fine, says the video, let us grant that the experience somehow comes out of the electricity. But then the firing of your neurons produces two things at once. One is the experience, the green square you see with your eyes shut. The other is the electrical signal going out to the next cell.

And here is the catch. Everything your body ever does is caused by that second thing. Pull your hand off the stove, say the words "I see a green square," write a book about consciousness: all of it runs on the electricity. If aliens landed and studied us, they could explain every single thing we do without ever guessing we have an inner life at all.

So the experience does nothing. It pushes nothing, triggers nothing, changes nothing. The video has a name for a thing like that: a ghost dragon. Imagine that every time electricity moves, an invisible dragon pops into existence, and it drifts through walls and never touches anything. If those dragons were real, nobody could ever find out. There is no way to learn about something that has no effect on anything.

But we do know about our inner experience. We talk about it constantly. We even know details, that the square has four sides and not five. So if nature could never have taught us, something outside nature must have. That is the conclusion: we were designed with that knowledge.

Where he is right

More than people will want to admit.

He is right that the electrical story is complete. There is no gap in the brain where a soul reaches in and flicks an atom. I am not going to argue for one.

He is right that your inner life does not fit into a physics report. You cannot hand someone a stack of measurements and have them taste what a green square is like. That is real, and it is not going away with better brain scanners.

And he is right about ghost dragons. If a thing genuinely has no effect on anything, you could never discover it. That rule is sound.

He is also right that most of the usual comebacks fail his test. He offers a good one: take your explanation of how experience relates to matter, hand the same explanation to the ghost dragons, and see if it helps. Usually it does not.

The one step I think is wrong: counting to two

Everything after that step follows fine. The problem is the step itself.

The argument says the firing produces two things: the experience, and the signal. Once you have said that, you have two separate items, one of them useless, and the rest of the argument is unstoppable.

But I do not think there are two things. I think there is one thing with an outside and an inside.

Think about a house

A house has walls. It also has an inside.

Nobody thinks the walls produce the inside as a second product, like exhaust. Nobody asks whether the walls or the inside is what keeps the rain off, as if they were competing. Having walls arranged that way is what it is to have an inside. One thing, two ways of talking about it.

And notice: you cannot find the inside by studying the bricks from the street. Not with better eyes, not with better instruments, not ever. Not because the inside is hiding, and not because it is made of ghost material. It is because looking from the street is the outside view, and the inside is the other one. To get it, you go in.

That is what I think experience is. Not something your brain gives off. The inside of what your brain is doing.

What I think you actually are

So far this is all a denial: there are not two things, there is one. That is not yet an answer, and the answer is the part that matters. Here it is in one sentence:

My consciousness is a dimensionless center surrounded by a field and articulated through a boundary. Through time, the center traces a one-dimensional line of continuity.

Four pieces. A middle with no size. A space around it. A skin. And a line drawn through time.

The middle has no size, and that is the whole answer to the dragon. A ghost dragon is a thing. It has wings, it has scales, it takes up room, it drifts through a wall. The argument takes a thing like that and says it has no effects, and then rightly points out that nobody could ever find it. But look at what kind of failure that is. A dragon is exactly the sort of item that should show up on a list of what the world contains. It has been specially excused from the list.

The middle of you is not like that. It has no size at all. It is not a tiny object sitting behind your eyes. It is the place everything else is arranged around, the way the center of a circle is not a dot you could pick up and weigh. You cannot find it by studying any one part of the edge, because it only exists as the thing the whole edge is pointing at.

So asking what work the middle does is like asking what the center of a circle weighs. It is not a light object. It is not an object. Nothing got stranded, because nothing extra was ever there.

The space around it is where the green square is. The video goes looking for a screen and cannot find one. The square is not on a screen. It is in the room, not on the wall. And a screen is for showing something to somebody outside; there is nobody outside. The square is not shown to you. It is had, from the middle.

The skin is where it gets said. The edge of you is where the inside becomes something that can be touched, measured and spoken. That is where the words come out, and it is what the alien robots are reading. Everything they measure is right.

The line is why you are still you. The middle keeps going, and the trail it leaves through time is your life. This answers something the video never asks but any account of experience owes an answer to: why are you a continuous someone, instead of a string of unconnected flashes that each blink out? Because it is the same middle, still there tomorrow. That is what memory and loyalty and being a person over time are made of.

Two ways of being one thing. This is the part I would most want him to hear. A list is one thing by including everything: it is one list, and everything is on it. A whole is one thing by being gathered: a space with a middle and an edge.

The robots' complete account is a list. They go down it asking which entry is the experience, find nothing, and conclude that the experience does nothing. But you are not a list. You are gathered. The experience is not one of the entries; it is all of it being gathered around a middle. Asking which item on the list is your experience is exactly like asking which brick is the inside of the house.

What this does not do. I am not claiming to have explained why any of this is lived. Saying what the shape of a point of view is does not prove that being that shape has to feel like anything. What it does is say where the feeling sits, and why it was never going to appear on the robots' list, and why its absence from that list is not a ghost sighting. That is less than a solution and a lot more than nothing.

And it comes with a bill I should be honest about. If having a middle and an edge and an inside is what it takes, then a great many things have some kind of inside, all the way down. I think that is true, and I would rather say it out loud than hide it. The hard part that follows is how the insides of the parts make the inside of the whole, and the short answer is that they do not add up: a person is not a crowd of cells wearing a coat, it is one gathered thing. Whether that fully works is an open question. It is a better open question than what to do about a dragon that was never born.

Now run his dragon test on it

He says: whatever you believe about experience and matter, try saying the same thing about ghost dragons and watch it fail. Good, let us try.

The dragon says: "I am not a creature that appears when electricity moves. I am what the electricity is like from the inside."

But then it has no wings. No scales. No size. It is not floating anywhere. It is not a dragon at all any more. It is not even a thing.

So the test does not break the answer. The test destroys the dragon. And that tells you something: the comparison only works if the second item is an object, some little creature that happens to be undetectable. An inside is not an object. Nobody asks how many scales the inside of a house has.

Here is another way to see the difference. You could delete the ghost dragons entirely and absolutely nothing would change. That is exactly what it means to say they do nothing. But you cannot delete the inside of a house and still have walls with a space in them. Take away the inside and there is no house, just rubble.

"But to us they would just be electrons"

The video sees a version of this coming. Maybe experience simply is the electricity, a package deal? He answers: that is like saying electrons are secretly tiny dragons. Even if it were true, we would never learn it. To us they would just be electrons.

That answer is good, and it works against what it is aimed at. It does not work here, and the reason is sitting inside his own sentence.

To us. From out here. Looking at it.

Yes. From the street, a house is a box. That is not news, and it is not a discovery that houses have no insides.

And when the question is your own experience, you are not out on the street. You are not trying to detect somebody else's inside from a distance. You are in yours.

So how do we know about it?

This is the heart of it, and I think the answer is simpler than the question makes it sound.

You never discovered your inner life. You never had to go and find it, and no signal ever had to travel from it to you. Discovery is what you do with things that are somewhere else. Your own inside is not somewhere else. You have been in it the entire time.

The rule he uses ("if it has no effect, nobody can learn about it") is a rule about finding things out there. It is a good rule. It just has nothing to say about the one case where the finder and the found are in the same place.

Then how can we talk about it?

Fair question, and the toughest one. Your mouth moves because signals move. Granted.

But think about a smile. A smile is not caused by amusement sitting next to it and giving it a push. The smile is what the amusement looks like from outside. One thing. Inside view, outside view.

Saying "I see a green square" is like that. The sentence is not a message smuggled out from a ghost. It is the outside of the very act that, from the inside, is the seeing.

And notice what this fixes. On the video's picture, the words and the experience are two separate items, and the words are caused entirely by the electricity. So your mouth would say "I see a green square" whether or not there was any experience at all, and it would just be an astonishing stroke of luck that it happens to be true. Every time. For everybody. Forever. That is a heavy price. One thing with two sides does not need the luck.

What about the alien robots?

They land, they scan us, they predict everything we will ever do, and they never suspect we have an inner life. The video treats this as proof that our inner life does nothing.

I would say something different. Nothing is missing from their predictions. Something is missing from their acquaintance. Those are not the same failure.

They are studying the house from the street. Every brick measured, every future creak predicted correctly. They have not been inside. Their instruments are not broken. They are just not us.

The monitor, and why a photo file has no inside

The video opens by saying a computer with no monitor cannot make a picture. A photo file is just characters. You need a screen to turn it into an elephant. Inside the brain there is no screen, so where is the picture?

Ask what a monitor is actually for. It is for showing a picture to somebody. So the question quietly assumes there is a little person in your head who needs a screen to look at. Then it points out that there is no screen. But there is also no little person. And if there were, you would need a second little person inside that one, watching their screen, forever.

You are not watching your experience. You are the watching. There is nobody it has to be displayed to.

On the photo file, though, he is right, and it matters. A file really has no picture in it. A file is marks on somebody else's surface. It has no inside of its own, no center, nothing it is doing on its own behalf. That is the actual difference between a file and you, and it is not about how much math is happening. It is about whether there is a whole thing there with an inside at all.

Which, honestly, is a live question about brains, and a live question about AI, and I am not claiming it is settled. But "is it computing hard enough" was never the right question to ask.

The part where the argument works against itself

Here is a difficulty that has nothing to do with my view. It is a problem the argument has on its own.

The whole thing rests on saying the electricity explains everything your body does. That is the step that strands the experience with nothing to do.

But talking about green squares is something your body does. So if something supernatural is what makes us talk about our inner lives, then the electricity was not explaining everything after all, and the key step is false. And if the electricity really does explain everything, then no supernatural source could have reached in to make us talk about it either, and we are exactly where we started.

Saying "God set it up at the very beginning" does not get around this. Setting it up still means making a physical difference. It just moves the difference earlier.

So it is a fork, and both sides hurt. Either the electricity explains all of it, and the conclusion is out of reach, or it does not, and the experience was free to matter all along.

So am I saying it is all just atoms?

No. I think he is right that there is more than what physics measures. I just do not think "more" means an extra invisible object stacked on top of the atoms.

Measuring is something you do from the outside. That is what measuring is. So the inside was never going to turn up on the list of measurements, and it not being on the list is not evidence of a ghost. It is what you would expect.

A note if you are religious

I am not writing this as a defense of "nothing exists but particles." I do not believe that either.

But look at what this argument costs you if you accept it. It says your inner life does nothing at all, and that God had to separately install the knowledge that you have one. That makes your experience an ornament, and the knowledge a patch on a design that did not work. A God who has to inform you that you have an inside is fixing a mistake.

I would rather say the inside was never an add-on. Being a whole thing is what having an inside means. Nobody needs to be told they are standing at their own center.

The short version

He counts two things where I count one. Everything else in the argument is fine, which is why it feels so tight. The dragons, the aliens, the unanswerable question about how we could ever know: all of it comes from that first count.

Fix the count and the dragons never hatch.

Nothing is missing from the robots' predictions.
Something is missing from their acquaintance.
You never discovered your own inside. You have been standing in it all along.

What I am sure of, and what I am not. The fork in the argument (either the electricity explains everything, or something outside it can reach in, and neither one gets you to the conclusion) is just a problem with the argument, and it stands no matter what you think of my view. That there is one thing with two sides, rather than two things, is my position, not a proof; it is an old idea with a long history, and this is a version of it, not a discovery. Whether wholeness rather than computation is the real dividing line, which is the interesting question for brains and for AI, is something I believe and cannot yet demonstrate. The four-part picture of what you are (a middle with no size, a space, a skin, a line through time) is an account and not a proof; it says where the inner life sits and why it was never going to be on the robots' list, and it does not show that being shaped that way has to feel like anything. And I am not claiming to have explained why there is any inner life in the first place. I think it is real, I think it cannot be boiled down to a third-person summary, and I am denying only the further claim that this makes it useless. Being impossible to reduce is not the same as doing nothing.

The same point in the framework's own words

For readers who know the notation: the firing is one ⊙, not a source of two products. Its boundary face (○) is the signal; its interior face (Φ, measured from •) is the experiencing. The report is the ○-face of the same measuring act, which is why report and experience agree without coincidence. The argument's opening move (experience as a separate emitted product) is the Severance Lie installed as a premise: the whole is treated as a mere point inside the greater whole, its interior stripped out, and then correctly observed to have nowhere to stand.
