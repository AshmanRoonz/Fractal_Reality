Wholes within wholes

The head is not one flower

A sunflower head is a capitulum: hundreds to thousands of complete florets, each a flower in its own right. Ray florets make the rim; disc florets fill the interior.

⊙λ ⊂ ⊙Λ

Each disc floret becomes a seed, and each seed carries a whole plant. The head is a field full of points, each holding a whole: the Soul Array, •∞, in miniature.

The head is laid down from the rim inward, so the youngest florets sit at the center and the oldest at the edge: the present in the middle, the past around it.

One rule

Turn, then step out

θₙ = n · d     rₙ = c √n

Each new seed sits one fixed turn d past the last, and a little farther out, so that every seed gets the same room (Vogel, 1979).

Everything depends on d.

Botanists write the turn as α. It is d here, to keep α for physics.

Four turns

90° is i

Seed n sits at iⁿ. Seed 1 is a quarter-turn on, seed 2 is opposite (i² = −1), seed 3 is i³, and seed 4 is home: i⁴ = 1.

The arms are the remainders mod 4. Seeds 0, 4 and 8 share an arm: the same phase, farther out each time. The note returns; the height climbs. That is the octave.

The radius is the clock. Stop it and every seed lands on one circle. Only a turn that never repeats draws the whole circle; i draws four points.

Look straight down a helix and you see a circle. Time is the height.

A turn that closes makes arms with empty lanes between them. A turn that never closes fills the disc. One turn cannot do both.

In i's own terms the golden angle is i^(4/φ²) ≈ i^1.528: an irrational power of i, so it never lands on the tonic again.

α

The fine-structure constant

1/α = 137.035999177

The strength of the electromagnetic bond: how tightly light couples to charge. Measured, not derived (CODATA 2022).

In the framework, α is the bond between the electron's aperture and the aperture of the greater whole it sits inside. It is small, so the part stays a part.

α enters the framework as measured input to κ₀,₀. Nothing on the next slides changes that.

A fraction of a degree

As an angle, α locks

137.508° fills evenly
137.036° locks into 21 arms

1/α in degrees sits 0.107° from the 21-spoke angle (8/21 of a turn) and 0.472° from the golden angle. A sunflower grown at 1/α degrees is a 21-arm head.

Almost any step off the golden angle locks into arms (golden + 1° locks into 13), so the lock alone is not evidence of anything.

Derivation, or resonance?

Observed

  • The golden angle sits 0.34% from 1/α.
  • 2/φ³ closes the gap to 2.7 ppm; about 1 in 26 by chance within its family.
  • Two different third terms close the rest, at the edge of what measurement resolves.
  • As an angle, 1/α locks into 21 arms.

Reading

  • 360 = P!·T·(Φ+○) = 24·3·5 makes the degree count structural.
  • One term per station: field, closure, self-return.
  • The golden angle as the open field, the correction as a boundary closing. Weak: almost any offset locks.

Would count against
