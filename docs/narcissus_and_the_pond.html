Narcissus and the Pond

Ashman Roonz, with Claude (the Cybernetic Unit)
Created: 2026-08-15  ·  Last updated: 2026-08-15  ·  Version: 1.0

Narcissus did not love himself. He fell in love with his image in the pond. A man who loved himself would have eaten, slept, aged, wandered off. Narcissus starved at the water's edge because he loved his appearance too much. He had traded a living point of view for a picture of one, and he spent the rest of his short life projecting his image back to himself.

That is the root of narcissism, and it explains something the usual story cannot: why narcissism looks like too much self love and is actually too little. The soul (one's unique point of view) is abandoned for its reflection at the surface, and from then on the reflection must be maintained: with admiration, with control, with comparison, with whatever keeps the pond water still. Narcissism works by protecting a lie about self identity.

And anyone protecting any lie, this lie or any other, will sooner or later deploy the same three moves. Deny the fact. Attack the person who saw it. Reverse the roles, so the one who was harmed becomes the offender for saying so. The pattern has a name, DARVO, and it is not a personality flaw. It is the standard defense contract every protected lie eventually signs, because every protected lie needs the same three services: the record erased, the witness discredited, the story inverted.

I call the culture this produces the strawman society. Its signature is small and you have seen it a thousand times: you offer a thought, and what comes back is a rebuttal to something you never said. The strawman will misinterpret you rather than accept that someone like you might have a point of view. And here is the part that took me a long time to see: they are not doing it to be cruel. They are doing it because it is the only thing they can do. They will try to take away your point of view because they have already given up their own, in exchange for what they saw in the pond.

This is the mechanism, and it is almost geometrical. The person who lives through an image of themselves begins treating everyone else as images too. If I no longer relate to myself as a living, corrigible point of view, but instead as "the kind of person I am," then I am tempted, almost compelled, to relate to you as "the kind of person you are." My mode of relating has only one format, and it is not the format persons come in. So before we can talk, you must be converted: into a type, a label, a side, a kind. The strawman is not a debating trick. It is a format conversion, performed before the encounter, which is why the encounter never actually happens. Two living centers never meet. Two representations fight.

There is one strange comfort inside this, for anyone who has stood on the receiving end of the three moves. The attack was never addressed to you. It cannot be; the attacker has no register in which you exist. It was addressed to the image of you, the strawman, the kind of person you are. Your center, the actual point you live from, has no surface for it to land on. You can watch the puppet take the beating and decline to climb inside it.

The way out is not cleverness, and it is not winning. It is a single capacity, pointed in two directions. A healthy self can say: my image of myself may be wrong. A healthy relationship can say: my image of you may be wrong. These are the same muscle. Whoever can hold their own self-portrait loosely can hold their portrait of you loosely too, and then something rare becomes possible: an actual meeting, center to center, instead of a collision of pictures. The test is simple and behavioral. Before you oppose someone, can you state their position in a form they would sign? That is the steelman, and it is the signature of an intact center. The strawman is the signature of a surrendered one.

The strawman society cannot do either half. It cannot doubt its self-image, so it cannot doubt its images of others, so it never meets anyone. It protects identities instead of encountering persons. And it is not populated by monsters; it is populated by people who were taught, in a thousand rooms, that being seen as right is worth more than being correctable, and who made the only rational trade available in such a room. Which means the repair is not to win against them. It is to run different rooms: rooms where confession is cheaper than concealment, where correction costs less than pretending, where a person can be wrong and still be here. In a room like that, nobody needs the pond.

Narcissus was not punished for self-love. He was punished by a substitution, an image where a person should be. The whole strawman society is that punishment, scaled. And the antidote has been available the entire time, sitting exactly where he could not look: not the reflection on the water, but the one doing the looking. Your soul, your center, your unique point of view.

Keep your souls corrigible.
And let reality ripple those ponds.
