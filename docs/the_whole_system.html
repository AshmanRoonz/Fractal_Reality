01 / BEGIN WITH WHAT EXISTS

Ontology before policy.

Everything exists as wholes, parts, and relationships. A person is not a component to be optimized away. Each person is an integrated whole participating in larger wholes.

•

The center: point of view

Each person has a distinct point of view. No two people occupy precisely the same perspective.

System requirement: Equal standing, freedom of thought, and meaningful voice.

Failure mode: A person is silenced or treated as expendable.

CORE RELATION
⊙ = Φ(•, —, ○)

In the Circumpunct philosophy, consciousness is the integrated whole of center, attention, boundary, and field. For social design, we use these roles as a structural analogy: a community is an organized whole of distinct people, without assuming that the community itself possesses a single conscious point of view.

A healthy whole preserves the wholeness of its parts.
The ethical bridge matters. Geometry does not, by itself, prove how humans ought to behave. This architecture explicitly assumes equal moral standing, non-domination, a duty to consider evidence, and procedures open to revision. The five rules below are proposed consequences of combining those commitments with the circumpunct's structure.
02 / THE FIVE CONSTRAINTS

A constitution for interaction.

These are not personality virtues that we hope people will possess. They are requirements the environment must protect, including when people disagree or behave badly.

•
0D / TRUE

Reality over rank.

Every assertion may be examined against evidence. No person's status turns belief into fact.

Non-substitution principle. High productivity does not excuse coercion. Good intentions do not excuse deception. Majority support does not erase a person's basic protections. Success in one dimension cannot automatically purchase failure in another.

Six rights every level must protect

Voice

The right to speak, ask, present evidence, and meaningfully contest claims.

Boundary

The right to privacy, consent, safety, and freedom from retaliation or coercion.

Exit

The right to decline participation or leave, subject to genuine existing obligations and others' rights.

Correction

The right to request review and have evidence weighed independently of status.

Due process

Notice, an opportunity to respond, proportionate consequences, and impartial appeal.

Dissent

The right to disagree without being forced to affirm a conclusion or punished merely for dissent.

04 / THE WHOLE AT EVERY SCALE

Recursive governance.

The same five principles apply to people, partnerships, teams, institutions, and society. Decision authority must be bounded, and every level must have a pathway for appeal.

SCALE 01 / INDIVIDUAL

A person is a whole, never merely a unit.

The first level begins with the person's own perception, boundaries, decisions, and capacity to revise beliefs.

Power and responsibilityFreedom of thought, consent, responsibility for actions, and freedom to revise beliefs.
Required processObserve → interpret → check evidence → choose → reflect.
Correction / appealSeek feedback, reconsider assumptions, and retain meaningful avenues for external support.
Failure to preventInternalized coercion, silencing, or treating certainty as proof.

Institutional machinery

A functioning system needs independent roles, not just admirable slogans.

01 / Open evidence commons

Track provenance, distinguish facts from interpretations, publish corrections, and protect confidential material.

02 / Boundary steward

Enforce consent, privacy, non-retaliation, proportional responses, and safe reporting channels.

03 / Decision assembly

Make choices through a predeclared rule, consult affected parties, and document rationales and objections.

04 / Independent review

Examine disputes and alleged rights violations without reporting to the same decision-maker under review.

05 / Correction log

Record what changed, why it changed, what harm was repaired, and when outcomes will be revisited.

06 / Anti-capture controls

Disclose conflicts, rotate oversight roles, protect whistleblowers, and allow challenges to the rules themselves.

05 / DESIGN FOR FAILURE, NOT FANTASY

Stress-test the rules.

A system cannot depend on everyone being truthful or kind. Switch safeguards off and see where predictable institutional vulnerabilities appear. This is a qualitative diagnostic, not a validated predictive model.

The hidden error

An employee discovers an error that could damage their reputation if reported. The organization needs accurate information to repair the harm.

Necessary response: Protect disclosure, investigate facts fairly, and separate honest mistakes from deliberate misconduct.

4 / 4 SAFEGUARDS PRESENT

All foundational safeguards are available.

The case still requires judgment and evidence, but the institution has the minimum procedures to respond without relying on personal virtue.

  • Watch for real-world failures of implementation, even when the rules look good on paper.
06 / FROM IDEA TO EVIDENCE

Build a small whole first.

We should try to falsify the proposal, not declare victory in advance. Begin in a willing group where participation is voluntary, the risks are low, and an independent comparison is possible.

PHASE 01 / SPECIFY

Write the charter.

Define membership, jurisdiction, boundaries, decision rules, appeals, record retention, and what happens when the system fails. Pre-register what success would mean.

PHASE 02 / PILOT

Run real decisions.

Use the Truth Resolution Protocol in a small voluntary team. Compare with an ordinary decision process and log both successes and harms.

PHASE 03 / REVISE

Correct the architecture.

Invite independent critics. Amend rules that fail, document the evidence, and expand only when protective and measurable benefits appear.

What to measure

M01 / DISCLOSURE

Are errors reported?

Rate of voluntary reporting, while accounting for whether more incidents are being discovered.

M02 / CORRECTION

Do facts change decisions?

Time to verify and correct errors; quality of evidence and responsiveness to new evidence.

M03 / PROTECTION

Can people speak safely?

Retaliation reports, privacy violations, willingness to dissent, and meaningful access to appeal.

M04 / FAIRNESS

Whose interests count?

Effects across different roles, including less powerful participants and people who choose not to agree.

M05 / COOPERATION

Does trust survive conflict?

Willingness to collaborate after disagreement without suppressing legitimate objections.

M06 / INTEGRITY

Can the rules be corrected?

Whether appeals succeed when warranted and whether independent oversight can actually change policy.

What would count against this design? If participants become less willing to disclose, minorities lose voice, safety worsens, power concentrates, or decisions become less accurate despite good-faith implementation, the architecture or its assumptions need revision. Corrigibility applies to this proposal too.
