# What Makes Harm Last

**Author:** Ashman Roonz

```
Created: 2026-08-01
Last updated: 2026-08-01
Version: 0.1
```

---

There is a war between truth and lies, and it is tempting to say it is the same war as the one between good and evil.

It is not quite, and the difference is where the useful part lives.

Harm happens without lying. Two people can understand a situation perfectly, both be honest about it, and still want incompatible things with not enough to go round. Someone loses and nobody deceived anyone. And the reverse happens too: people cause terrible damage while sincerely believing they are helping, which is an error and not a lie.

So the two wars are not one war.

But something joins them, and it is more specific than a slogan.

Harm that is seen clearly tends to get corrected. Not always, and not quickly, but the people it falls on can name it, and the people causing it can be shown what they are doing, and some of the time that is enough. Correction is the ordinary way things stop being wrong.

Harm that lasts is different. Harm that lasts is being kept from view.

And the thing keeping it from view is the lie.

That is what a lie is for. Not to make someone believe a false sentence, which is trivial and usually harmless, but to disable the mechanism by which a wrong would otherwise be found and fixed. A working lie does three things at once: it distorts what is happening, it hides the fact that it has distorted anything, and it teaches people to hear correction as an attack. That third part is the important one. It is why more evidence does not help, and why the people best placed to notice are the ones most reliably discredited.

So the honest version of the claim is not that evil is falsehood. It is that **no structural harm persists without a lie protecting it.**

That is a smaller claim and a far more useful one, because it tells you where to look.

## Lies are not a character problem

The obvious response is that we need more honest people.

This has never worked and cannot work, and it is worth being clear about why.

People lie where lying is cheaper. Not the worst people, most people, and reliably. Put an ordinary person in a place where telling the truth ends their career and lying is rewarded with promotion, and most of them will not become heroes. Some will. Most will look away, and then find a reason why looking away was reasonable, and then, in time, come to believe the reason.

This means the distribution of lying in a society is not mainly a fact about its people. It is a fact about its incentives. It is a gradient, and gradients are built. Whistleblower protection is a gradient. Whether a junior person can contradict a senior one is a gradient. Whether an audit is performed by someone the audited party can punish is a gradient.

The most dishonest institution I can imagine would not need a single liar in it. It would only need an arrangement in which the truth was expensive at every level, and ordinary people doing ordinary self-preservation would produce the same result as a conspiracy, without anyone ever deciding to deceive.

## Why a liar at the top needs everyone else

There is a structural fact here that is easy to miss.

Nobody can claim to be the only real whole by themselves.

To be the centre that everything else exists to serve, you need other people occupying the position of mere part. And a person with an intact sense of their own wholeness does not take that position voluntarily. So the claim always requires a second party who has been persuaded, pressured, or raised to believe they have no centre of their own.

This is why such arrangements spend so much of their energy on what people believe about themselves, rather than only on what people do. The belief is not a side effect. It is the foundation. A leader who lies is held up by something, and what holds him up is a large number of people who have agreed, mostly without noticing, to be less than they are.

But the usual word for those people is wrong, and the error matters.

We call them empty, hollow, dupes. That picture cannot explain what we actually see, because what we see is not apathy. It is energy. Enormous, sustained, often joyful energy.

What has been lost is not connection. Connection is at maximum. What has been lost is autonomy: the capacity to remain a distinct centre while participating. Highly connected and no longer their own. That combination produces devotion, not emptiness, and devotion is what makes these movements strong.

It also happens by increments small enough to accept. Ignore one contradiction. Excuse one lie. Silence one doubt. Accept one violation of a boundary you would once have defended. Each step is small and the next is smaller than the distance already travelled, and belonging becomes conditional on the next surrender. Eventually the person demands the same surrender from newcomers, because the hierarchy has become their safety and defending it is now self-defence.

There is a second group that is genuinely different, and confusing the two produces bad responses. Some people are not connected to anything and also have no stable centre. They belong to nowhere and are nobody in particular. They are not holding anything up. They are the recruitment pool, and what they need is not correction but somewhere to belong that does not charge autonomy at the door.

## The trap in saying any of this

Everything above can be said by anyone about their opponents, and it usually is.

Every polarised society contains two groups each convinced that the other is composed of dupes propping up liars, and each is describing the other with total sincerity. That is not a coincidence and it is not a symmetry to be smug about. It is a warning about the shape of the argument itself.

The rule I try to hold is that the party being graded does not get to choose the axis. If my analysis exempts me, it is not an analysis, it is a weapon. And the version of this essay that identifies who the strawmen are would be exactly that.

So the question is not who is lying.

The question is what truth costs, and for whom.

## What to measure

This is the part that can be checked, which is why I think it is the only part worth arguing about.

A society is not made honest by its stated values, its founding documents, or the sincerity of its leaders. It is made honest by what happens to the person with the least power in the room when they say something true and inconvenient.

That is a number. It shows up in retaliation rates, in what happens to whistleblowers three years later, in whether the junior person who contradicted the senior one is still employed, in whether the inconvenient study was funded again.

Where that number is bad, you will get lying regardless of anyone's virtue, and the harms already present will last, because nothing can reach them.

Where that number is good, harm becomes visible, and visible harm is the only kind that gets fixed.

## What this does not promise

Making truth safe does not make peace.

It makes peace possible, which is different and smaller and still worth everything. Genuine agreement needs more than truth: it needs boundaries, so that refusal is real; it needs the relation to be rightly ordered; it needs the arrangement not to consume one party for another's benefit; and it needs the commitments to still matter afterwards. Truth is one of the conditions, not the whole set.

But it is the one the others depend on, because without it the parties do not know what they are agreeing to.

And it is the one that decides whether anything else can be repaired, because a harm nobody is permitted to name will simply continue, patiently, for as long as it is protected.

Peace becomes possible when it is safer for the least protected person in the room to say something true and inconvenient than to say nothing at all.

Everything else is downstream of that.

## Filing notes (Claude, 2026-08-01)

Drafted from the session's closing exchanges. Structure and argument are Ashman's across four statements: that there is a war between truth and lies and it is the same as good and evil; that peace arrives when truth is safer than lying; that we live in a society of strawmen holding up lying leaders; and the corrections he accepted to each.

Three things were changed from the originals during drafting, all of them discussed and agreed in session:

**The identity claim is weakened on purpose.** "The war between truth and lies is the war between good and evil" fails in both directions: harm without lies (tragic conflict, honest error) and lies without harm. What survives is the mechanism, that no structural harm persists without a lie protecting it from correction, which is smaller and tells you where to look.

**"Strawmen" is replaced by the wholeness plane's diagnosis.** The word implies hollowness and cannot explain the energy these movements have. High connection with autonomy surrendered explains it. The essay also separates the second population, low connection and low autonomy, which is not holding anything up and needs a different response.

**The axis problem is made a section rather than a caveat.** The essay would otherwise be an instance of the thing it describes, since every polarised society contains two groups each certain the other is composed of dupes. Naming this in the body rather than in a footnote is what keeps the piece usable by someone who disagrees with the author about who the liars are, which is the only condition under which it does any work.

**Open for Ashman:** whether the closing measurement should carry concrete examples with sources (retaliation statistics, whistleblower outcome studies) or stay as stated. Sources would make it checkable; they would also date the essay and pull it toward a specific national context, which the draft currently avoids.
