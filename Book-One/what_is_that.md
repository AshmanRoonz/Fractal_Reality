My mother told me that my first persistent question was:

> "Ossat?"

*What is that?*

I asked it constantly. I pointed toward particular things and wanted them named. What is that object? That sound? That animal? That person? Each question drew a boundary around something and distinguished it from everything surrounding it.

Eventually, the question expanded.

I stopped asking only:

> What is **that**?

And began asking:

> What is **this**?

What is this experience? What is this world? What is consciousness? What is Reality? What is all of this, taken together?

The movement from **that** to **this** is the movement from identifying a part to confronting the whole.

"That" points away from the center toward something distinct.

"This" includes the one pointing, the thing being indicated, and the relation between them. It includes the present moment in which Reality and an experiencer meet.

Perhaps philosophy begins there:

> There is Reality, and there are those of us who experience it.

The first question asks what the things within Reality are. The deeper question asks what this entire relationship is.

My lifelong question has therefore remained almost unchanged. It only grew with me:

> **Ossat?**
> What is that?
> What is this?
> What is all of this?

The Circumpunct Framework is my attempt to answer.

There is infinite Reality and there are indivisible centers of experience. Each center meets Reality from a particular point of view. Mind mediates that encounter, body gives it an interface, and the continuing relationship becomes a conscious life.

And perhaps the question already contains the beginning of its answer.

I asked:

> What is this?

Years later, a nonverbal child answered my question about the meaning of life with one word:

> **This.**

My first question was *What is this?*

The answer that eventually returned was simply:

> **This.**
