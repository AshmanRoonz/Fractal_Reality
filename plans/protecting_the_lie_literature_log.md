## 1. The finding that changes the thesis

**Thomaes, S., Bushman, B. J., Orobio de Castro, B., Cohen, G. L., & Denissen, J. J. A. (2009). Reducing narcissistic aggression by buttressing self-esteem: An experimental field study. Psychological Science, 20(12).**

A randomized field experiment with 405 young adolescents: a short self-affirmation values-writing assignment reduced narcissistic aggression for about a school week. ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02478.x), [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19906123/))

This bears directly on the thesis's sharpest discriminator (discrimination table row 5; the Steele prediction), which held that domain-irrelevant self-affirmation should FAIL to reduce defensiveness in the narcissistic range. The pre-registered checklist item ("confirm the Steele affirmation paradigm has not already been run against narcissism; the result goes in either way") has now returned an answer, and the answer is prima facie AGAINST the strong form of Prediction 5.

Scope limits of the finding, which determine what survives: subclinical trait narcissism in adolescents, not the pathological range; generic ego threat, not documented contradiction of a specific elicited self-claim; outcome was aggression, not belief revision or witness discrediting; affirmation administered before threat. The thesis's row 5 is therefore rewritten (v1.4) from a clean discriminator into a boundary-condition test: the PLM survives only if affirmation's protective effect disappears (a) in the pathological range, (b) under claim-diagnostic contradiction rather than generic insult, or (c) on revision/discrediting outcomes rather than aggression. If affirmation also attenuates defense under those conditions, row 5 is conceded to the self-affirmation account and the PLM loses its sharpest test. This is recorded as a receipt, not buried.

## 4. Novelty assessment (preliminary)

Searches for an existing academic functional reanalysis of "narcissistic supply" found: the construct's popular inflation, one call to reposition supply as a "serious social psychoanalytic concept," and a general observation that the construct "has lacked serious academic scrutiny in the last twenty years." No confirmation-based respecification of supply, no exemption-from-revision model of narcissistic identity, and no equivalent of the PIP construct surfaced. The thesis's novelty claim SURVIVES this preliminary check, with two cautions: (a) web search is not a systematic review; PsycINFO/Scopus searches with controlled vocabulary are still required; (b) the components are all precedented (Morf & Rhodewalt's self-affirmation goal, NARC's grandiosity maintenance, valence-over-validity, blame externalization); what appears novel is the assembly (exemption from revision as the defining condition, supply as perimeter report, corrigibility as the treatment target) rather than any single part. The thesis should claim exactly that and no more.
