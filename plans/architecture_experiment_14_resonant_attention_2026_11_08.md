## 1. What this tests, and what it does not

The note proposed that attention is not only pointing but tuning: a selective coupling between a center and part of the field, amplifying some patterns and letting others fade, as a string answers some frequencies and not others. It proposed four tests: that a coherent whole develops a collective rhythm; that some parts couple more strongly to a stimulus; that the region of coupling is a movable focus; and that the whole can shift its focus by changing its internal organization. Experiments 12 and 13 already answered these for a world of still cues, under other names: the ring's heading is the phase of its first mode, the heading law θ' = (εw/V) sin(φ − θ) is a locking law, credibility weights were the selective coupling, re-lock time was the moving focus, and the gain set corrigibility. What the organism lacked was time: its parts had no rhythm, its cues did not move, and nothing in it could be in or out of tune.

This experiment gives the ring a clock and makes the cues rotate, and asks the note's question as the theory would put it:

1. **Does the whole have a rhythm that no part has?** A ring that reads its neighbours unequally carries a turn that rotates at a derived rate. Each part's activity then rises and falls at that rate, though no part has a frequency of its own. A list has no turn and so no rhythm; a node's loop has one; a leader latches.
2. **Is the ring's attention a tongue?** A rotating cue is followed only if its rate lies within a band around the ring's own rate, whose half-width is the heading law's gain ratio K = εw/V. The gain sets the band: the fast ring has a wide tongue, the stiff ring a narrow one.
3. **Does selection by frequency need a coupled whole?** With two cues of equal weight rotating in opposite directions, a tuned ring follows one and ignores the other; the list, with no clock, reports their bisector and follows neither; an untuned ring faces a standing wave and follows neither.
4. **Can the whole shift its focus from inside?** By a change of its coupling (the experimenter moves the clock), and by a rule of its own that moves the clock toward whatever pulls on the heading (the adaptive frequency rule). A list has nothing to tune.
5. **What does holding mean when the object moves?** In Experiments 12 and 13 the 0 held a still heading through blank trials. A moving target must be held by keeping time, and keeping time accumulates every error of rate. The derivation says a distractor outside the tongue still pulls the mean rate, so the hold is short, and shorter for the fast ring, which the distractor captures.

What this does not test. The parts are the same parts as before, with no rhythm of their own; the note's Kuramoto proposal, in which each part is an oscillator and the collective rhythm is their agreement, is a different hypothesis (rhythm in the parts rather than of the whole) and is left for a later experiment if this one passes. Nothing here learns weights (Experiment 13), and nothing bears on experience, on a soul, or on the canon's placement of attention, which is a 0 in the 1 (convergence, the center's side) while resonance belongs to the field; the note's triad of direction, continuity and attunement spans three stations, and this experiment tests its resonance reading inside the Theory of Wholeness thought experiment without touching canon. The string's harmonics already model the nesting of scales in the corpus; here the organism is a closed string whose first mode is its attention.

## 2. What changes from Experiments 12 and 13, and what stays

**The clock.** The ring reads its two neighbours with weights ½ − β on the left and ½ + β on the right (Experiment 12 used ½ and ½). For a first-mode pattern z_j = r e^{i(ψ_j + θ)} the reading is the pattern multiplied by C = cos(2π/n) + 2iβ sin(2π/n) = |C| e^{iχ}, so the recurrent drive leads the pattern by χ = atan(2β tan(2π/n)) and the pattern rotates. The node's clock is the same device in its loop: the hub's broadcast to the parts is rotated by χ_h, I_j gains κ_out Y e^{i(ψ_j + χ_h)}. The list has no coupling and so no clock.

**The cues.** Cue c is a direction that rotates, φ_c(t) = Ω_c t + α_c, continuous in time across trials (α_c uniform per seed), entering as before, I_j = Σ_c ε w_c e^{i(ψ_j + φ_c(t))}, ε = .3 per cue. Weights are fixed and equal unless a test says otherwise; nothing is learned. The target is cue 0, the resource; a hit is the heading within 20° of it at the trial's end, as before.

**The self-tuning rule (Test 3b and 3c only).** β' = η_β · pull, with pull = Im(conj(P̂) · Î) / V, where P̂ is the normalized population vector, Î = (1/n) Σ_j I_j e^{−iψ_j} the input's first mode and V the heading law's stiffness (V(G) for the ring, 5.9 for the chair, the value Experiment 13 measured). For one cue, pull = (εw/V) sin(φ − θ), the same quantity that drives the heading. η_β = .004 / (2 tan(2π/n)) = .00483, so that the clock rate ω₀ moves at η_ω = .004 times the pull. The chair's rule is χ_h' = η_χ · pull with η_χ = .008 (dω_h/dχ_h ≈ ½). This is the adaptive frequency rule of Righetti, Buchli and Ijspeert (2006) stated for the whole; a local form (each part shifting its own left and right weights by the slip between its drive and its activity) is for later.

**What stays.** n = 16, the leak and limit law, dt .05, σ .05, trials of 40 time units (800 steps), the boundary on (the cues are patterned; the uniform pull is closed since Experiment 12), 100 seeds, the organisms L, N chair (G_h 0) and leader (G_h 1.5) with β = .5, κ_in 1, κ_out .5, G_p 4, and R at G ∈ {2, 4, 8}. State carries across trials and nothing resets. The heading is sampled every 5 steps.

## 3. Measures

Per seed and trial, scored over the trials a test names:

- **ω_meas**: the heading's rotation rate from its unwrapped samples (rad per time unit).
- **F_c**: the fraction of samples within 45° of cue c (within reach of the cue).
- **LOCK_c**: the order parameter |mean exp(i(θ − φ_c))| over the trial's samples (1 when the relative phase is steady, small when it slips; no heading counts 0). **Locked** means LOCK ≥ .97 (one cue); **follows** means the seed's mean LOCK over the scored trials exceeds .8 (two cues, where the other cue makes a locked heading wobble).
- **hit_c**: the heading within 20° of cue c at the trial's end.
- **clock**: β (as ω₀ = 2β tan(2π/n)) or χ_h (as ω_h = tan(χ_h/2)) at the trial's end.
- **Winding** of every ring at every trial.

## 4. Derivations

**The clock.** With the first-mode ansatz, the limiter preserves each part's phase, so a pure first mode stays one. Writing the pattern's complex amplitude a = r e^{iθ}: r' = −r + tanh(G|C|r) cos χ and θ' = tanh(G|C|r) sin χ / r. At the resting amplitude tanh(G|C|r*) = r*/cos χ, so **θ' = tan χ = 2β tan(2π/n)**, exact and independent of G: β = .0604, .1207, .2415 give ω₀ = .050, .100, .200 rad per time unit, with lags χ = 2.9°, 5.7°, 11.3°. The resting amplitude falls by cos χ and |C| rises, and the heading law's stiffness V_β = G|C|r* equals the old V = G r cos(2π/n) to within 0.01% at |β| ≤ .25: the clock changes the rate and nothing else. For the node, the parts' pattern follows the rotated broadcast with lag atan(ω) and the hub's pattern follows the parts with lag atan(ω) (each stage relaxes at rate 1), so a steady rotation needs χ_h = 2 atan(ω_h): **ω_h = tan(χ_h/2)**; χ_h = .1993 gives .100. This is a reduced-model derivation (the saturating stages are treated as first-order phase followers) and carries a ±25% band.

**The tongue.** With a cue at rate Ω, the heading law with the clock is θ' = ω₀ + (εw/V) sin(φ − θ − χ). Writing ψ = φ − θ, **ψ' = (Ω − ω₀) − K sin(ψ − χ), K = εw/V**: Adler's equation. It locks if and only if |Ω − ω₀| ≤ K, at a lag ψ = χ + asin((Ω − ω₀)/K); outside, the relative phase slips at the beat rate √((Ω − ω₀)² − K²). K = .173, .081, .041 at G = 2, 4, 8 (ε .3, w 1), and .051 for the chair at V 5.9. The time to lock from a relative phase ψ₀ at zero detuning is (1/K) ln(tan(ψ₀/2) / tan 22.5°): from 170°, 19, 41 and 81 time units at G 2, 4, 8, a half trial, one trial and two trials.

**Hits and reach follow the lag.** A hit needs |χ + asin(δ/K)| ≤ 20°, so the hit tongue at β = .1207 is δ ∈ [−.075, +.043] at G 2, [−.035, +.020] at G 4, [−.018, +.010] at G 8, asymmetric because the clock's own lag χ eats into one side. Reach (45°) is wider: [−.134, +.109], [−.063, +.052], [−.031, +.026]. So a ring locked near the edge of its tongue attends to a cue it does not hit; the three measures are kept apart for that reason.

**Two cues.** Two cues of equal weight rotating at ±.10 sum to a standing wave: amplitude 2 cos(Δ/2) along the fixed bisector of their start phases, flipping by 180° when they are more than 180° apart. The list's heading is this bisector (its leaky filter only attenuates each cue by 1/√(1 + Ω²) and lags it by atan Ω, equal for the two), so it is within 45° of either cue exactly half the time and locked to neither. A ring tuned to one cue has the other as a periodic force at relative rate .2, outside every tongue, which makes the locked heading wobble by K/.2 radians: 50°, 23°, 12° at G 2, 4, 8, and 15° for the chair; this is why the fast ring follows the target with poorer reach and fewer hits than the stiff one. A ring with its clock at zero faces the standing wave itself and settles on the bisector, following neither cue.

**Frequency pulling and the hold.** A force outside the tongue still shifts the mean rate of the driven phase toward itself by δ − √(δ² − K²) (the slipping phase lingers where the force opposes the slip). When the target is absent and the distractor alone remains at detuning .2 from a clock exactly at the target's rate, the heading's mean rate is pulled by .099, .017, .0042 rad per time unit at G 2, 4, 8 (.0043 for the chair), so the heading falls 45° behind the absent target after 8, 45 and 190 time units: a fifth of a trial, one trial, five trials. The adaptive rule adds a second effect in two directions: it erodes the clock toward the distractor at η K ⟨sin⟩ (3.97e−4, 6.9e−5, 1.7e−5 rad per time unit squared), and, because it integrates the pull, it gives the phase inertia that filters the fast distractor and reduces the pulling; the reduced model has both and section 5 reports its numbers. The ring's own noise contributes a random walk of 5° per trial and 14° over ten, negligible beside the pulling.

**Self-tuning.** Outside the tongue the time-averaged pull is K ⟨sin ψ⟩ = δ − √(δ² − K²) toward the cue, so the clock drifts toward the nearer cue at rate η times that, reaching the tongue's edge and then locking; inside the tongue the pull is K sin ψ at the locked lag and the clock converges at rate about η K per unit of lag. From a clock at .02 (detunings .08 to the target and .12 to the distractor): the target is already inside the G 2 and G 4 tongues (K .173, .081), so they lock at once and tune within about 250 time units (six trials); the chair (K .051) drifts at 2.8e−5 per time unit and reaches its tongue's edge after about a thousand time units (26 trials); the G 8 ring (K .041) drifts at 1.6e−5 and does not reach the target within 30 trials. The stiff clock that holds best tunes slowest, by the same K.

**The list and the leader.** The list follows any single cue at any rate (its filter has no tongue) and no cue when two compete. The leader commits to its first heading and never moves (Experiment 12), so it follows a still cue and nothing that rotates.

**The reduced model.** `experiments/architecture_experiment_14_reduced_v1.py` integrates the heading law with the clock through every test below: one angle per organism, the list as the angle of the filtered cue sum, the chair as the ring law at V 5.9 with its own clock, the leader fixed. It has no parts, no limiter and no hub. It was used, in this order: to pick the cue rates (±.10, so that the distractor lies outside every tuned tongue and the untuned ring faces a standing wave), the reach window and the lock threshold, the self-tuning rate η (.004, so that the fast and middle rings tune within twenty trials and the stiff ring does not), and the hold design (clocks tuned, the rule on and off); then to produce the numbers below at 500 seeds. Every number carries a band: ±.05 for the list and the rings, ±.10 for the chair.

## 6. What would count against the framework, and what against the design

Against the readings:

- **K1.** The list follows one cue and not the other (seed mean LOCK > .8 on exactly one cue) in ≥ .3 of seeds in Test 2: selection by frequency needs no coupled whole, against P2.2.
- **K2.** A ring with its clock at .10 follows the target in < .8 of seeds at any G: the Adler reading of the heading law fails under rotation, against P2.1.
- **K3.** The G 2 ring with its clock at zero follows one cue in ≥ .3 of seeds: a standing wave can be chosen between, against P2.3.
- **K4.** Self-tuning sends any organism to the distractor in > .2 of seeds: the rule does not tune toward the nearer pull, against P3.2.
- **K5.** With the rule on, the G 8 ring holds less than the G 4 ring (lower F_target on trials 33 to 35): the stiffness reading of holding fails, against P3.3.
- **K6.** The chair, with its measured clock within .02 of .10, follows the target in < .5 of seeds in Test 2: the node's loop is not a clock, against P2.1.

Against the design, which would mean recalibrating before anything is claimed:

- **K7.** The ring's measured rotation in Test 1a is off 2β tan(2π/n) by more than 10% at any β: β is reset from the measured rate for Tests 1b to 3 (rule stated now) and P1.1's ring clause is marked MISS.
- **K8.** The chair's measured rotation at χ_h .1993 lies outside [.075, .125]: χ_h is reset from the measured ratio for the later tests and P1.1's chair clause is marked MISS.
- **K9.** The correspondence check (the full ring at clock .10, one cue at .10 and at .30, G 2 and G 4) differs from the reduced model by more than .05 in F or LOCK: the reduced model's bands are reported as failed and the run continues.

## 9. Prior art the result will be read against

The locking equation and its tongue are Adler (1946) and Arnold's tongues; the standard account is Pikovsky, Rosenblum and Kurths (2001). A bump that rotates because the ring's weights are asymmetric is the velocity-integration mechanism of head-direction models (Skaggs and colleagues 1995; Zhang 1996). The self-tuning rule is the adaptive frequency oscillator of Righetti, Buchli and Ijspeert (2006). Attention as an entrained oscillation is dynamic attending theory (Large and Jones 1999); selection by coherence of rhythms is Fries (2005, 2015), and entrainment as sensory selection is Lakatos and colleagues (2008) and Schroeder and Lakatos (2009). A ring state with a turn is a twisted state (Wiley, Strogatz and Girvan 2006). All of these are from memory and unchecked for this document. Within the series: the heading law and the 0 are Experiment 12; the gain's price on every change is Experiment 13; the note that prompted the design is the Circumpunct Life author's, 2026-11-08.

**A related model, checked the same day.** The Life author's own implementation of the note (`circumpunct_resonant_attention_study.zip`, README titled "Experiment 4, Attention as Selective Resonance") represents a string by its first two modes, drives both with the same two-tone input, smooths each mode's amplitude into a sensed value s_i, and feeds the two values to two attention units with mutual inhibition (w 1.2) and slow adaptation (τ_h 15). It reports a median of 16 switches of dominance per 320 time units with both tones present and unchanged, none without adaptation, no single focus without inhibition, and the present tone selected when one tone is removed. Its numbers match its shipped metrics, and its own caveats name the cause (inhibition plus built-in habituation). A reimplementation of its equations (`resonant_check.py`, session scratchpad) reproduces every row and adds the test the README does not run: over the scored window the string's two outputs are constants (.7992 ± .0028 and .7910 ± .0051), and replacing them by those two numbers, or by one equal number, leaves every switch count and selective fraction unchanged; halving or doubling τ_h gives 27 and 8 switches. So the string is a fixed pre-filter that supplies two nearly equal constants, the selection is the inhibition, and the switching is the adaptation: a two-unit rivalry oscillator (Laing and Chow 2002; Shpiro and colleagues 2007; from memory). In the theory's terms it is a list of two channels with a gate, the boundary's function, and no center; nothing in it is tuned to a frequency, which is why this experiment's selection is placed in the clock of the turn instead. Its suggested next step, an internal state that maintains focus against competition and corrects toward a goal, is Experiment 13's credibility weights and this experiment's self-tuning rule.

**A second related model, checked the same day.** The author's `circumpunct_experiment_6_learned_resonance.zip` (README titled "Experiment 6, Learned Resonant Attention") keeps the two string modes as labels only (its README says no wave equation is solved; in the code the two channels are a 2 by 2 permutation matrix of ones) and asks whether a controller with two resource stores can learn which channel refills which store from its own outcomes, and relearn after a secret reversal at step 235. It reports the learner at .7066 on the weaker resource after the change against an oracle at .7173, equal splitting at .6916 and a learner frozen at the reversal at .2240, recovery from a shock in 9 steps against 19, and a 99.4% correct mapping after the reversal with a median relearning latency of 16 steps. **Reading, before the fact (sections 1 and 5), as it stands.** The ring's clock is exact and costs nothing in stiffness. Attention by resonance is a tongue set by the gain, and selection among equal, simultaneous, counter-rotating cues is done by every coupled organism in every seed and by the list in none: the list has no clock and reports the bisector. An untuned coupled organism follows neither. A still clock cannot attend to what moves faster than its tongue, whatever the weight, which the list, having no tongue, can. A moved clock re-locks in one to two trials; a self-tuning clock finds the nearer cue and never the farther; the stiff clock tunes slowest, holds longest, and the fast clock is captured by what remains and stays captured.

**After the fact.**
- The chair's clock runs at .77 of the derived rate. The derivation treated the two stages of its loop as first-order phase followers; the saturating stages lag more. The band of 25% let it stand, and every chair clause that depends on lag then failed (hits .00, reach .52, locked at .05 and .10 only, the hold) while every clause about selection passed (follows 1.00, 0, 1.00 across the three clocks). The band was set for the clock, not for the hit margin it feeds; a band of 10% would have reset it.
- With the rule on, the chair corrected its own clock to a measured .103 and reached F 1.00 before the blank. The self-tuning rule compensates a mis-derived loop clock; a tuning whole does not need its clock derived right.
- The fast ring's turn breaks under a cue that slips fast past it (one cue at Ω .3: winding kept in 36% of seed-trials; 7% lost at clock 0 under two cues). The reduced model has no turn to lose, which is what K9 caught. The fast ring is at once the most corrigible, the most distractible and the most fragile; all three follow from the same K.
- The lag angle's sign matters for reach with two cues: in the reduced model the G 2 ring reaches .75 at +5.7°, .85 at 0 and .99 at −5.7°. The reduced model did not update the angle at retune, which produced the frozen .99 for P3.1; the full ring's .82 lies between the correct-sign values. The plan's "follows" criterion (LOCK > .8) also differs from the reduced model's tuned criterion (reach > .6), which matters only for slow slipping (G 8 in Test 3b). Both are defects of the derivation tool, now recorded.
- Holding a moving target is a matter of trials, not of ten trials: the ordering stiff > middle > fast is exact, the pulling by a distractor outside the tongue does what the derivation said, and the rule that erodes the clock also lengthens the stiff ring's hold (.96 against .57 on trials 33 to 35) by giving the phase inertia.

**What this adds to the Theory of Wholeness, pending Ashman's reading.** The 0 can keep time: the turn's rotation is a rhythm of the whole that no part has. Attunement, selection by rate, belongs to any coupled whole with a clock and to no list; it is a different mechanism from the gate (selection by inhibition, the boundary) and from learned weight (Experiment 13). The gain that bought persistence in Experiment 13 buys selectivity here, and the same gain makes the fast ring distractible and fragile. The leader latches and has no rhythm.
