# The Corrigibility Statement

**Status: session record, 2026-08-21. Ashman's reflection on the forum-ban history, recorded as an adjudication-grade insight about the framework's communication layer, with the statement he wished he had led with drafted in two lengths, a candidate principle staged as a §25.17/§25.20 refinement, and placement decisions staged. Pending countersign.**

## 1. The reflection (Ashman, verbatim in substance)

Looking back on attempting to post in forums like r/consciousness and getting banned for the presentation: the writing carries full conviction, as if the author believes every sentence to be the truth. That is not the author's epistemic state; the register is a method, chosen so the ideas can be considered seriously. But shipping the register without the state was a disservice to readers, because they could not see the corrigibility behind it.

## 2. The diagnosis, in the corpus's own terms

- **§25.17 applied to epistemic status:** the Resolution Protocol promises the receiver control of the aperture. A transmission that carries first-order content at full resolution and second-order state (how the author holds it) at zero resolution gives the receiver nothing to control. Epistemic status is part of the signal, not packaging.
- **§25.20's cell structure:** the old posts were not lies and not paltering; every first-order sentence was honestly meant as hypothesis. But the absent second-order channel let readers' inference land in the wrong cell, and an absence that predictably misleads is a resolution error even when no sentence is false.
- **The loss theorem (countersigned this date):** the reader's record R of the author was the post; corrigibility was in X, but the post's partition never distinguished it, so H(corrigibility | post) was total. Leading with the statement is literally widening the partition: making the author's actual state an accessible distinction.
- **The register itself is not the error.** Spinoza's geometric method, the Tractatus, and the corpus's own Theme and Variations all state their themes at full volume; the last is honest because A Note on Honesty travels with the theme. The error is conviction traveling alone.

## 3. The candidate principle (staged for adjudication)

> **Conviction is a register; corrigibility is a state. A transmission that shows the register and hides the state misinforms the reader about the author, even when every sentence about the world is honestly meant.** Therefore the confident form and the corrigibility disclosure are one unit: neither ships without the other.

Staged as a refinement riding under §25.17 (epistemic status is part of the transmission's resolution, and the receiver is owed it first, since it is the layer that tells them how to hold everything after it) and §25.20 (the visible second-order channel as the public face of lived corrigibility).

## 4. The statement, drafted

**Short form (leads a post; the two-sentence version):**

> Before the confidence: I write this in a declarative voice because half-said ideas cannot be engaged or killed, and I want this one to be killable. I hold all of it as hypothesis; the public record includes my retractions, and if you show me a real error, the work changes and your correction becomes part of it.

**Full form (front matter for pages and papers):**

> **How to read the confidence.** I write in a declarative register because a framework stated at half-volume cannot be tested, and I want this one tested. The register is a method of exposure, not a report of certainty. My actual position: this is a hypothesis I have spent years refining, which has survived some checks and failed others, and the failures are on the public record with dated retractions that stay visible. Its quantitative claims carry stated bands and named kill conditions; some of them may die on schedule, and the record will say so. If you show me a real error, I will retract in public and credit the correction. That is the standing deal: full conviction in the writing, full corrigibility in the practice, and receipts for both.

**Optional method-disclosure line (Ashman's call, venue-dependent):**

> This work is developed in collaboration with an AI partner; every claim is audited against the working record, and the audit trail, including the claims the process killed, is public in the repository.

The optional line exists because the same invisible-state problem applies to method: in venues that care about AI involvement, an undisclosed method is another distinction the reader's record cannot resolve, and the corpus's FAITHFUL and TRUE argue for widening that partition too where it is material.
