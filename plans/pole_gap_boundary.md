## 1. Why this document opens with a warning rather than a claim

**A pattern that appears only under an unfair comparison is not an emergence. It is an artifact of the observer you built.**

The Pole Gap framework must apply to its own instruments before it is applied to reality. A hash is not a state. A threshold is not a floor. A first crossing is not a discovery. An oracle-selected model is not a fair observer. In each case a compressed marker was mistaken for the fuller condition it was meant to indicate.

This is not ceremonial rigour added after the fact. The protocol in §6 was generated by five actual failures, all of them in this program's own instruments, all of them inside a single day:

| # | the marker | what it stood in for | what it invented | caught by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a pair of 32-bit hashes | the full substrate row | a cycle at step 9,637 that did not exist, with a period | the instrument (contradicted an independent computation) |
| 2 | EMA below 0.3 bits/step | H(Y\|C_k) = 0 | a floor "found" at k = 12 where k_min = 21 | the instrument (the k-sweep) |
| 3 | the online rule's accuracy | the model class's Bayes accuracy | an understatement of the order-12 limit (89.6% for 92.646%) | Ashman |
| 4 | the first budget where losses cross | a persistent crossing | a crossover at n = 1,000 where ΔH = 2e-5 | the instrument, in its own output |
| 5 | min over k of realized loss | a fair fixed-order observer | a seductive architecture flip across projections | the instrument, before reporting |

Every one has the same form: **compressed indicator ≠ thing indicated.**

## 2. The general statement

The program began as a claim about randomness: it is relative to a projection and an observer, never a property of a sequence. Applied reflexively it yields a claim about instruments.

Let Z be a condition of interest and T(Z) an instrument's marker. The instrument is itself a projection, Z → T(Z), and therefore carries its own pole gap. An inference fails exactly when

> Z₁ ≠ Z₂ and T(Z₁) = T(Z₂),

that is, when the instrument's many-to-one map collapses a distinction the conclusion depends on. All five failures above are instances.

The response is not to abandon indicators, which is impossible, but to characterise what each one destroys and to verify conclusions with a less destructive measurement wherever one exists. In this corpus's own vocabulary the failure mode is the Inflation Lie at instrument scale: a part claiming to be the whole. Its signature is the one the framework predicts, confident assertion of a closure that is not there.

## 4. Prior art, stated so the boundary is not flattering

The program does **not** introduce any of the following, and any presentation implying otherwise is false.

- **Computational mechanics** (Crutchfield and collaborators, late 1980s onward). Causal states, the ε-machine as minimal optimal predictor, Markov order, cryptic order and crypticity, exact and asymptotic synchronization, and transient information and synchronization time *specifically for periodic sequences*. The ring-15 setup is this literature's subject. k_min is a Markov order. τ_sync is synchronization time.
- **Context-tree methods.** CTW (Willems, Shtarkov, Tjalkens 1995) mixes all bounded-memory tree sources, attains Rissanen's redundancy lower bound, and handles unbounded depth in linear time, with redundancy decomposed into coding, parameter, and model terms. Variable-length Markov models already exist as sparse alternatives to full high-order models with dimension growing in n. "Different model organisations pay different complexity costs" is theirs.
- **Growing-order model selection.** Shibata (1980) and the subsequent infinite-order AR literature already let the approximation order grow with sample size, balancing approximation error against estimation cost, with second-order prediction error decomposed into goodness-of-fit and complexity terms. The exponential-decay-gives-log-n classification is the known shape here.
- **Predictive coarse-graining.** State space compression already optimises macroscopic descriptions for predictive accuracy against computational cost; predictive rate-distortion compresses causal states while preserving future information; observer-class comparisons under restricted partitions already exist.

So "projection affects prediction," "architecture affects cost," and "optimal order grows with data" are all established territory.

## 5. The residual claim

Narrowly stated, and not a guaranteed novelty claim until the literature search is broader:

> Holding the substrate and the predicted quantity fixed, does changing the observation projection reverse the relative prequential performance of two observer architectures, after both pay fairly for model selection?

And, as a program rather than a result: one operational framework in which projection loss, representational limit, exact prequential learning cost, and observer architecture are measured **separately** on a **shared** substrate. The neighbouring literatures each hold one axis fixed. This is a coordinate system and an instrument between territories, not a new continent.

## 6. Protocol for the next experiment

**Fair comparator.** Replace the oracle min-over-k with a prequential mixture over fixed orders, Q_FO = Σ_k w_k Q_KT,k. Both arms then pay for structural uncertainty: one mixes uniform-depth models, the other variable-depth trees. Same maximum depth D, compatible priors, and D beyond k_min for the hardest projection so no optimum sits at the truncation.

**Statistic.** A_π(n) = L_FO,π(n) − L_CTW,π(n). The test is the *interaction*, I(n) = E_aggregate[A_π] − E_algebraic[A_π], not the sign of any single projection.

**Mechanism made measurable.** Do not test the labels. Define V_π as the weighted spread of the shortest adequate suffix length across contexts, and regress A_π on V_π with entropy rate and difficulty as controls. The hypothesis is γ₁ > 0: the advantage grows with depth heterogeneity.

**Prospective projections.** Construct the aggregating and algebraic families in advance (majority and density at varying radius and threshold; XOR and mod-2 linear at varying support and spacing). Do not relabel the five that were already run: the labels were generated from that result and testing them on it would be the same error the program keeps catching. Match the families on entropy rate, marginal balance, minimum useful depth, and best achievable loss, so "CTW wins on easier sequences" cannot masquerade as an interaction.

**Replication.** One Rule 30 initial row is one sequence; slicing it does not create independent replicates. Use random initial rows, several ring widths, varied offsets and burn-in. Report paired A_π,r with intervals.

**Persistence rule, pre-registered.** A ranking counts only when the interval excludes zero across a specified budget interval. Report the whole A_π(n) curve against log n; a crossover n_{FO→CTW,π} is itself informative.

**Instrument verification, done.** All four CTW checks pass to machine precision, including exact agreement with brute-force enumeration over every permitted tree at depths 1 to 3. See `experiments/pole_gap_ctw_validation_v1.py`.

## 7. How this fails

Any of these is a real result: no reliable interaction appears; aggregating projections do not produce greater depth heterogeneity; heterogeneity does not predict the advantage; the interaction vanishes under difficulty matching; the fixed-order mixture performs equally well everywhere.

## 8. The standard

The Pole Gap is not validated when it explains the observer's errors. It is validated when it helps the observer discover that the error was its own.
