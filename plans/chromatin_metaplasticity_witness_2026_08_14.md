# Witness Note: Chromatin Metaplasticity and the Resonance Doctrine

**Status:** external witness note, filed 2026-08-14 at Ashman's direction. A consonance record, not a confirmation: the source is a preprint (not yet peer reviewed), in mouse, with the causal question open. Revisit on publication.

## The finding

A Stanford preprint (Yuxi Ke; Greenleaf and Schnitzer labs) tracks engram neurons in mouse medial prefrontal cortex for a month after fear conditioning, using activity-dependent tagging and single-nucleus multiome sequencing. Reported results: engram cells acquire a new chromatin landscape that is faint at 7 days and strong at 28; at recall, these cells do not simply boost classic plasticity genes but rewrite their transcriptional response, preferentially engaging chromatin regulators, RNA processing, and protein turnover; the late chromatin state is enriched for embryonic developmental transcription-factor motifs. The authors' framing: chromatin metaplasticity: the nucleus stores rules for how the neuron will respond at the next recall, not just traces of the past.

Sources checked 2026-08-14: Ke is a real Stanford researcher (Knight Initiative Brain Resilience seminar listing); the Schnitzer lab publications page carries the corresponding work (chromatin rearrangements during remote memory storage altering the transcriptional control logic of engram neurons). Lineage, which the viral framing erased: engram chromatin priming was shown in 2020 (Marco et al., Tsai lab), and chromatin plasticity predetermining neuronal eligibility for memory traces was published in Science (adg9982). This work extends a known line; it is not an overnight revolution.

## Struck riders

The viral post carrying the preprint attached two claims the paper does not touch, struck here so they are never cited from this note: (1) organ-transplant memory (not an established phenomenon; anecdote; neuronal chromatin in mPFC says nothing about organs carrying autobiography); (2) generational trauma (the study is within one organism, within neurons, over 28 days; germline transmission in humans is contested science outside this paper's scope). The post is a case study in resolution abuse: a real finding wrapped in unmarked inflation, unquotable as a unit.
