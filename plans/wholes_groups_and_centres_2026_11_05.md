## The four adjudications, in Ashman's words

1. On "There is not always another whole.": "wrong"
2. On "there is no greatest whole": "true and false, god is an unbounded whole, we can call god the greatest whole, but in the mereology of wholes and parts, there is no top whole"
3. "groups are wholes, and i suppose there are many types of centers"
4. On souls being ultimate: "an observer is ultimate to itself, at least"

## What each one changes

**1. There is always another whole.** This retracts the second half of the compact line in Finding 3 of 2026-08-08, "There is always more Reality. There is not always another whole." The first half is untouched. It also removes the problem behind Finding 5 of that record, since a whole above a person has a referent again. Finding 4's options for `⊂[α]` lose much of their motive, since containment by a next whole holds again. Whether connection also runs sideways, Finding 2's shared field, is not contradicted, and Ashman has not withdrawn it.

**2. God is an unbounded whole, and the chain has no top.** Two statements now stand together. In the mereology of wholes and parts there is no top whole, which is what "there is no greatest whole" was protecting. And God may be called the greatest whole, as an unbounded whole. This qualifies "∞ is one without being whole" (2026-08-01, Finding 4; `CLAUDE.md`), which said wholeness requires a boundary. The existing line that the step from ⊙Λ to ∞ is categorical and not vertical still fits, since an unbounded whole is not the next rung.

**3. Groups are wholes, with many types of centre.** This reverses Finding 1 of 2026-08-08 as stated, "a group is not a whole", though not its observation that a fuzzy set of goals is divisible and a point is not. That difference moves from whole against not-a-whole to one type of centre against another. So the 2026-08-08 migration queue for the social rows of the domains table is void: Person ⊂ Society stands, as `CLAUDE.md` already has it.

**4. Ultimate means ultimate to itself, at least.** Souls are not asserted to be the bottom of the chain, which fits "circumpuncts all the way down". The claim is first-person, and "at least" leaves open whether there is more.

## What follows and is not yet settled

- **The ethics needs restating.** Finding 1 said the failure is "a group with no soul claiming the standing of a whole". With groups as wholes that becomes a whole with one type of centre claiming the standing of another type, such as an organisational centre claiming the standing of a centre of experience. The argument looks recoverable in that form. Ashman has not adjudicated the wording.
- **`⊙_A + ⊙_B ⇏ ⊙_C` (2026-08-08, graded B+) rested on Finding 1.** If groups are wholes, two wholes in relation may form a third of some type. This needs a decision.
- **Finding 7's consistency check** read the World as the largest group, with no centre. It now needs rereading as a whole of some type, and not as a top.
- **Which types of centre carry experience** is now the live question, and the tests cannot reach it. The uploaded statement "The Circumpunct: A Theory of Wholeness" says as much in its section 2.

## Types of centre the tests have actually distinguished

Synthetic models on wiring Claude drew. None of the numbers change under these adjudications, only what the types are called.

| Type | What it is | Nodes that follow and can move | Cut in two | Its centre cut away |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Barycentre | The mean of a list, a description | 0 in a long list, all in a short one | As many wholes as pieces | Nothing to cut |
| Several occupants | A few nodes that all read | As many as occupy, while few | One whole for each piece with an occupant | The others carry on |
| Point | A node with no interior, read by all | 1 | One whole | It and its parts fade |
| Chair | The same, with an interior, still follows | 1 | Not run | Parts fade, it holds |
| Leader | A stronger interior, does not follow | 0 | Not run | Parts fade, it holds |
| 0 | What a closed turn of phase forces, not a node | 0 | 1 and 0, or 0 and 0 | Nothing to remove |

**A correction to something Claude said.** On 2026-11-05 Claude told Ashman that a field with no boundary has no net turn, and that this leaned toward "there is no greatest whole". That holds only for a closed field such as a sphere. An unbounded open field has no such limit, so that argument gives no support either way, and Claude withdraws it.
