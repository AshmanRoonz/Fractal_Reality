# The Odd-Cycle Balance-Rigidity Theorem

**Status:** derived and proved, 2026-08-20, v1.2. This note strengthens the group-only-at-balance result commissioned in plans/countersign_batch_2026_08_19.md. It generalizes the framework's seven-station case, proves eleven equivalent characterizations of balance, classifies the closure generated by every rational and irrational imbalance, and derives exact geometric and Fourier defects. The mathematics is elementary circle-group, finite-Fourier, and additive-combinatorial mathematics. No literature novelty is claimed. **The framework interpretation is COUNTERSIGNED (Ashman, 2026-08-21, "Yes. Signed"; disposition 7 in `plans/countersign_batch_2026_08_21.md`).**

## 0. Abstract

Take \(m\) structural intervals. Put one process station at offset \(b\in(0,1)\) inside each interval, and identify the final process station with the next tonic. The resulting cycle has

\[
N=2m+1
\]

distinct stations and circumference

\[
L=m+b.
\]

Then \(b=1/2\) is uniquely characterized by each of the following: equal spacing, additive closure, nontrivial rotational symmetry, zero circular centroid, and unitarity of the coordinate-induced Fourier matrix. At balance the stations are exactly the cyclic group \(\mathbb Z_N\).

Off balance, the visible station set is strictly smaller than the group it generates. If \(b=a/q\) is rational in lowest terms, its additive closure is

\[
\mathbb Z_{mq+a},
\]

which has more than \(N\) states. If \(b\) is irrational, the generated orbit is infinite and dense in the coordinate circle. Thus balance is the unique minimal finite closure of the staggered construction.

For the framework, \(m=3\), \(N=7\), and balance gives \(\mathbb Z_7\).

## 1. Construction

Let \(m\ge1\) be an integer and \(b\in(0,1)\). Define the full ordered cycle

\[
\mathcal O_{m,b}
=
(0,b,1,1+b,\ldots,m-1,m-1+b,m,m+b\equiv0').
\]

Its circumference is

\[
L=m+b.
\]

Its \(N=2m+1\) distinct stations are

\[
S_{m,b}
=
\{0,1,\ldots,m\}
\cup
\{b,1+b,\ldots,m-1+b\}
\subset
C_L,
\]

where

\[
C_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z.
\]

The framework octave is the specialization \(m=3\):

\[
S_{3,b}=\{0,b,1,1+b,2,2+b,3\},
\qquad
L=3+b,
\qquad
N=7.
\]

## 2. Symmetric-Window Lemma

**Lemma 1.** In the coordinate circle \(C_L\), the staggered station set has the equivalent representation

\[
S_{m,b}
=
\{-m,-m+1,\ldots,-1,0,1,\ldots,m\}
\pmod L.
\]

**Proof.** Since \(b=L-m\), every process station satisfies

\[
j+b=j+L-m\equiv j-m\pmod L.
\]

As \(j\) runs from \(0\) to \(m-1\), these are the residues \(-m,-m+1,\ldots,-1\). The structural stations supply \(0,1,\ldots,m\). QED.

**Consequences.** The visible stations are always invariant under the reflection

\[
x\mapsto-x.
\]

The parameter \(b\) changes the circumference of the circle around a fixed symmetric integer window. This is why the circular moments below become Dirichlet kernels.

## 3. Main Balance-Rigidity Theorem

Define the first circular moment

\[
M_1(m,b)
=
\frac1N
\sum_{s\in S_{m,b}}
e^{2\pi i s/L}.
\]

Order the stations as \(s_0<s_1<\cdots<s_{N-1}\) in the displayed representative interval, and define the coordinate-induced Fourier matrix

\[
(F_{m,b})_{kr}
=
\frac1{\sqrt N}
e^{2\pi i r s_k/L},
\qquad
0\le k,r<N.
\]

**Theorem 2, balance rigidity.** The following statements are equivalent:

1. \(b=1/2\).
2. \(b\) is self-complementary: \(b=1-b\).
3. \(L=N/2\).
4. All \(N\) consecutive gaps of \(\mathcal O_{m,b}\) are equal.
5. The gap variance is zero.
6. The single sum \(b+b\) belongs to \(S_{m,b}\) in \(C_L\).
7. \(S_{m,b}\) is a subgroup of \(C_L\).
8. Translation by \(b\) permutes \(S_{m,b}\).
9. A nonidentity rotation of \(C_L\) maps \(S_{m,b}\) to itself.
10. The first circular moment vanishes: \(M_1(m,b)=0\).
11. The coordinate-induced Fourier matrix \(F_{m,b}\) is unitary.

When these statements hold,

\[
S_{m,1/2}
=
\frac12\mathbb Z
\big/
\frac N2\mathbb Z
\cong
\mathbb Z_N.
\]

### Proof

**Conditions 1 to 5.** The consecutive gaps are

\[
b,1-b,b,1-b,\ldots,b,1-b,b,
\]

with \(m+1\) gaps of length \(b\) and \(m\) gaps of length \(1-b\). They are all equal exactly when \(b=1-b\), which gives \(b=1/2\). A finite list has zero variance exactly when all of its entries are equal. Also

\[
L=m+\frac12=\frac{2m+1}{2}=\frac N2
\]

at this value, and the converse follows from \(L=m+b\).

**Condition 6.** Since \(0<b<1\), no wrap occurs in \(2b\). The only possible station equalities in the relevant range are

\[
2b=b,
\qquad
2b=1,
\qquad
2b=1+b,
\]

where the last candidate exists only when \(m\ge2\). These give \(b=0\), \(b=1/2\), and \(b=1\), respectively. Only \(b=1/2\) lies in the allowed interval. Conversely, at balance \(2b=1\in S_{m,b}\).

**Conditions 7 and 8.** If \(S_{m,b}\) is a subgroup, it contains \(b+b\), so Condition 6 applies. If translation by \(b\) preserves \(S_{m,b}\), it sends \(b\) to \(2b\), so Condition 6 again applies. At \(b=1/2\), the stations are all half-integer multiples modulo \(N/2\), so they form \(\mathbb Z_N\), and translation by \(b\) is its one-step cyclic permutation.

**Condition 9.** At balance, the stations are a regular \(N\)-gon and admit \(N\) rotational symmetries. Off balance, the cyclic gap word contains \(m+1\) copies of \(b\) and \(m\) copies of \(1-b\), with the two lengths unequal. A nontrivial rotational symmetry would make this word repeat \(r>1\) times. Then \(r\) would divide both multiplicities \(m\) and \(m+1\), which is impossible because they are coprime. Thus the rotational stabilizer is nontrivial exactly at balance.

**Condition 10.** Let

\[
z=e^{2\pi i/L}.
\]

Lemma 1 gives

\[
N M_1(m,b)
=
\sum_{j=-m}^{m}z^j
=
\frac{\sin(N\pi/L)}{\sin(\pi/L)}.
\]

The denominator is nonzero. Therefore \(M_1=0\) exactly when \(N/L\) is an integer. But

\[
2-\frac1{m+1}
<
\frac NL
<
2+\frac1m,
\]

and the only integer in this open interval is \(2\). Hence \(L=N/2\), so \(b=1/2\). The converse follows immediately.

**Condition 11.** At balance,

\[
s_k=\frac k2,
\qquad
L=\frac N2,
\]

so

\[
(F_{m,1/2})_{kr}
=
\frac1{\sqrt N}e^{2\pi i kr/N},
\]

the unitary \(N\)-point DFT matrix. Conversely, if \(F_{m,b}\) is unitary, its constant column and first-harmonic column are orthogonal. Their inner product is \(M_1(m,b)\), so Condition 10 gives \(b=1/2\). QED.

## 4. Symmetry-Enhancement Corollary

**Corollary 2.1.** The full circle-isometry stabilizer of \(S_{m,b}\) is

\[
\operatorname{Iso}(S_{m,b})
\cong
\begin{cases}
C_2, & b\ne1/2,\\
D_N, & b=1/2.
\end{cases}
\]

**Proof.** Lemma 1 supplies the reflection \(x\mapsto-x\) for every \(b\). Off balance, Theorem 2 excludes every nonidentity rotational symmetry. A second reflection would compose with the first to produce such a rotation, so exactly one reflection remains. At balance the regular \(N\)-gon has dihedral symmetry \(D_N\), of order \(2N\). QED.

Balance therefore enhances an always-present mirror symmetry into full cyclic and dihedral symmetry. It does not create symmetry from nothing.

## 5. Closure-Explosion Theorem

Theorem 2 says when the visible set is already a group. The next result classifies the larger group generated when it is not.

Write

\[
\langle S_{m,b}\rangle
\]

for the additive subgroup of \(C_L\) generated by the visible stations.

**Theorem 3, closure explosion.** Exactly one of the following holds.

### Rational balance parameter

If

\[
b=\frac aq,
\qquad
1\le a<q,
\qquad
\gcd(a,q)=1,
\]

then

\[
L=\frac{mq+a}{q}
=
\frac pq,
\qquad
p=mq+a,
\]

is already in lowest terms, and

\[
\langle S_{m,b}\rangle
\cong
\mathbb Z_p.
\]

At balance, \((a,q)=(1,2)\), so \(p=N\) and the visible \(N\) stations fill the generated group. At every rational off-balance value, \(q\ge3\) and

\[
p=mq+a
\ge
3m+1
>
2m+1=N.
\]

Thus every rational imbalance embeds the \(N\) visible stations in a strictly larger finite cycle.

### Irrational balance parameter

If \(b\) is irrational, then

\[
\langle S_{m,b}\rangle
\]

is infinite and dense in \(C_L\).

### Proof

By Lemma 1, every station is congruent to an integer, and \(1\in S_{m,b}\). Therefore

\[
\langle S_{m,b}\rangle=\langle1\rangle
\]

inside \(C_L\).

For rational \(L=p/q\) in lowest terms, the least positive integer \(n\) satisfying \(n=kL\) for some integer \(k\) is \(n=p\). Hence the rotation by \(1\) has order \(p\). Since

\[
\gcd(mq+a,q)=\gcd(a,q)=1,
\]

the displayed fraction for \(L\) is reduced. The balance and off-balance size statements follow from the possible denominators.

For irrational \(L\), no positive integer multiple of \(1\) returns exactly to zero in \(C_L\). To see density, normalize the circle to unit circumference and write \(\alpha=1/L\). By the pigeonhole principle, for every \(\varepsilon>0\) some nonzero integer multiple of \(\alpha\) lies within \(\varepsilon\) of an integer. Choose its sign so its fractional part is \(\eta\in(0,\varepsilon)\). The points

\[
0,\eta,2\eta,\ldots,\left\lfloor\frac1\eta\right\rfloor\eta
\]

have every consecutive gap, including the final gap to \(1\), at most \(\eta<\varepsilon\). They belong to the two-sided orbit generated by \(\alpha\), so that orbit is an \(\varepsilon\)-net for every \(\varepsilon\), hence dense. QED.

**Framework examples, \(m=3\).**

| \(b\) | \(L=3+b\) | Generated closure | Visible fraction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| \(1/3\) | \(10/3\) | \(\mathbb Z_{10}\) | \(7/10\) |
| \(2/5=0.4\) | \(17/5\) | \(\mathbb Z_{17}\) | \(7/17\) |
| \(1/2\) | \(7/2\) | \(\mathbb Z_7\) | \(7/7\) |
| \(3/5=0.6\) | \(18/5\) | \(\mathbb Z_{18}\) | \(7/18\) |

| irrational \(b\) | irrational | infinite dense orbit | \(7/\infty\) |

The \(0.4\) and \(0.6\) octaves can cancel seam drift when placed consecutively, but their local additive closures are different: \(\mathbb Z_{17}\) and \(\mathbb Z_{18}\). Global seam cancellation does not restore either local group.

## 6. Exact Sumset Growth

Let \(kS\) denote the set of sums of \(k\) elements of \(S=S_{m,b}\). Since \(0\in S\), this also contains every sum using at most \(k\) nonzero terms.

**Theorem 4.** If \(L=p/q\) is rational in lowest terms, then

\[
|kS|
=
\min\{p,\,2km+1\}.
\]

If \(L\) is irrational, then

\[
|kS|=2km+1.
\]

**Proof.** By Lemma 1, the \(k\)-fold sumset has the integer representatives

\[
\{-km,-km+1,\ldots,km\}.
\]

For rational \(L=p/q\), two integers represent the same point of \(C_L\) exactly when their difference is a multiple of \(p\). A consecutive interval of \(2km+1\) integers therefore has \(\min\{p,2km+1\}\) residues. For irrational \(L\), no nonzero integer difference is an integer multiple of \(L\), so all representatives are distinct. QED.

**Corollary 4.1.** At balance,

\[
|S+S|=|S|=N.
\]

At every off-balance value,

\[
|S+S|>N.
\]

For irrational \(b\), the exact value is

\[
|S+S|=2N-1.
\]

Thus exact algebraic closure is brittle. An arbitrarily small generic imbalance expands the two-fold sumset from \(N\) states to \(2N-1\), even though the metric and Fourier defects below remain arbitrarily small.

**Corollary 4.2.** In the rational case, the least sum depth that fills the generated group is

\[
k_{\rm sat}
=
\left\lceil\frac{p-1}{2m}\right\rceil.
\]

Balance is uniquely the case \(k_{\rm sat}=1\).

## 7. Quantitative Metric Defects

Exact group closure changes discontinuously. Geometry changes continuously. The following formulas quantify that stable side.

### 7.1 Gap variance

**Theorem 5.** The population variance of the \(N\) gaps is

\[
V_{\rm gap}(m,b)
=
\frac{m(m+1)}{N^2}(2b-1)^2
=
\frac{4m(m+1)}{N^2}
\left(b-\frac12\right)^2.
\]

**Proof.** The mean gap is

\[
\mu=\frac LN=\frac{m+b}{N}.
\]

The two gap deviations are

\[
b-\mu
=
\frac{m(2b-1)}N,
\]

and

\[
1-b-\mu
=
-\frac{(m+1)(2b-1)}N.
\]

Weighting their squares by multiplicities \(m+1\) and \(m\), then dividing by \(N\), gives the result. QED.

For \(m=3\):

\[
V_{\rm gap}(3,b)
=
\frac{12}{49}(2b-1)^2.
\]

### 7.2 Displacement from the anchored uniform subgroup

The coordinate circle \(C_L\) always contains the anchored uniform order-\(N\) subgroup

\[
H_{m,b}
=
\left\{
0,\frac LN,\frac{2L}N,\ldots,\frac{(N-1)L}N
\right\}.
\]

Let

\[
\delta=b-\frac12
\]

and compare the ordered station \(s_k\) with \(kL/N\). Define

\[
e_k=s_k-\frac{kL}{N}.
\]

**Theorem 6.** For \(0\le j\le m\),

\[
e_{2j}
=
-\frac{2j}{N}\delta,
\]

and for \(0\le j<m\),

\[
e_{2j+1}
=
\frac{2(m-j)}{N}\delta.
\]

Moreover,

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e_k=0,
\]

\[
\operatorname{RMS}(e)
=
\frac{2}{N}
\sqrt{\frac{m(m+1)}3}
\,|\delta|,
\]

and

\[
\max_k|e_k|
=
\frac{2m}{N}|\delta|.
\]

**Proof.** Substitute

\[
s_{2j}=j,
\qquad
s_{2j+1}=j+b,
\qquad
\frac LN=\frac12+\frac\delta N.
\]

The sums follow from the standard formulas for \(\sum_{j=1}^m j\) and \(\sum_{j=1}^m j^2\). QED.

**Corollary 6.1.** The gap variance and the anchored position error satisfy the exact identity

\[
\operatorname{RMS}(e)^2
=
\frac13V_{\rm gap}(m,b).
\]

For \(m=3\), the full displacement vector is

\[
(e_0,\ldots,e_6)
=
\frac\delta7
(0,6,-2,4,-4,2,-6),
\]

with RMS \(4|\delta|/7\) and maximum \(6|\delta|/7\).

### 7.3 A one-sum closure witness

Let \(d_L\) be geodesic distance on \(C_L\), and define

\[
d_{+}(b)
=
\min_{s\in S_{m,b}}d_L(2b,s).
\]

**Theorem 7.** For every \(m\ge1\),

\[
d_+(b)
=
\min\{b,1-b,|2b-1|\}.
\]

In the central regime \(1/3\le b\le2/3\),

\[
d_+(b)=2\left|b-\frac12\right|.
\]

The defect vanishes inside \((0,1)\) exactly at balance.

**Proof.** If \(b\le1/2\), then \(2b\) lies between the consecutive stations \(b\) and \(1\), so its nearest-station distance is

\[
\min\{b,1-2b\}.
\]

If \(b\ge1/2\) and \(m\ge2\), then \(2b\) lies between \(1\) and \(1+b\), giving

\[
\min\{2b-1,1-b\}.
\]

For \(m=1\), the same second expression comes from the stations \(1\) and \(0\) across the wrap, since \(L-2b=1-b\). Combining the two regimes gives the stated minimum. On \(1/3\le b\le2/3\), the term \(|2b-1|\) is minimal. QED.

The endpoint behavior needs care: \(d_+(b)\) also approaches zero as \(b\) approaches \(0\) or \(1\), because stations collide in those excluded degenerate limits. Gap variance is the better global metric across the full open interval.

## 8. Fourier-Rigidity Theorem

For an integer \(r\), define the normalized circular moment

\[
a_r(m,b)
=
\frac1N
\sum_{s\in S_{m,b}}
e^{2\pi i r s/L}.
\]

**Theorem 8.** For \(r\ne0\),

\[
a_r(m,b)
=
\frac{\sin(N\pi r/L)}
{N\sin(\pi r/L)}.
\]

At a removable zero of the denominator, the quotient is understood by continuity.

The Fourier Gram matrix has the exact Toeplitz form

\[
G_{m,b}
=
F_{m,b}^*F_{m,b},
\qquad
(G_{m,b})_{pq}=a_{q-p}(m,b).
\]

The matrix \(F_{m,b}\) is invertible for every \(b\in(0,1)\), but it is unitary only at \(b=1/2\).

**Proof.** Lemma 1 turns the moment sum into the Dirichlet kernel

\[
\frac1N\sum_{j=-m}^m e^{2\pi i rj/L}.
\]

The Gram identity follows by taking column inner products. For invertibility, let

\[
u_k=e^{2\pi i s_k/L}.
\]

The nodes \(u_k\) are distinct, and \(F_{m,b}\) is a normalized Vandermonde matrix in those nodes, so its determinant is nonzero. Unitarity is Theorem 2. QED.

**Corollary 8.1, exact Fourier leakage.** Define

\[
\Lambda_F(m,b)
=
\|G_{m,b}-I\|_F.
\]

Then

\[
\Lambda_F(m,b)^2
=
2\sum_{r=1}^{N-1}
(N-r)
\left[
\frac{\sin(N\pi r/L)}
{N\sin(\pi r/L)}
\right]^2,
\]

and

\[
\Lambda_F(m,b)=0
\iff
b=\frac12.
\]

**Corollary 8.2, signed first-harmonic balance signal.** The first moment is real and obeys

\[
\operatorname{sgn}M_1(m,b)
=
\operatorname{sgn}\left(\frac12-b\right).
\]

**Proof of the sign.** The denominator \(\sin(\pi/L)\) is positive. If \(b<1/2\), then \(2<N/L<2+1/m\le3\), so \(\sin(N\pi/L)>0\). If \(b>1/2\), then \(2-1/(m+1)<N/L<2\), an interval contained in \((3/2,2)\), so \(\sin(N\pi/L)<0\).

Near balance,

\[
M_1(m,b)
=
-\frac{4\pi}
{N^2\sin(2\pi/N)}
\left(b-\frac12\right)
+
O\!\left((b-1/2)^2\right).
\]

For the framework case \(N=7\), the linear coefficient is approximately

\[
-0.32802023.
\]

The gap variance measures the magnitude of imbalance quadratically and without direction. The first circular moment detects the side of balance linearly.

**Corollary 8.3, unique optimal conditioning.** The spectral condition number satisfies

\[
\operatorname{cond}_2(F_{m,b})\ge1,
\]

with equality exactly when \(b=1/2\).

**Proof.** Equality of the condition number means all singular values of \(F_{m,b}\) are equal to a common value \(\sigma\). Every column has norm \(1\), so

\[
\|F_{m,b}\|_F^2=N=N\sigma^2,
\]

which forces \(\sigma=1\). Hence \(F_{m,b}\) is unitary, and Theorem 2 gives \(b=1/2\). The converse is immediate. QED.

## 9. What Happens to the Existing \(\mathbb Z_7\) Harmonic Results

The theorem forces a two-register distinction.

### Abstract label register

The seven ordered labels can always be called \(j=0,\ldots,6\). If the ordinary label DFT is applied to those indices, the quadratic traversal sequence remains a CAZAC chirp and the linear splitting sequence remains a pure tone for every \(b\), because that calculation depends only on labels modulo \(7\).

### Coordinate-induced register

Only at \(b=1/2\) do those labels coincide with the coordinate-translation group of the seven physical stations. Off balance:

1. The coordinate Fourier matrix remains invertible, so information is not lost.
2. It is no longer unitary, so its modes are not orthogonal and its inverse is not its adjoint.
3. Noise amplification is governed by the condition number of \(F_{3,b}\).
4. At rational \(b=a/q\), the complete coordinate group is \(\mathbb Z_{3q+a}\), not \(\mathbb Z_7\).
5. At irrational \(b\), there is no finite coordinate group; the generated orbit is dense.

Therefore the precise conclusion is not "harmonic analysis disappears off balance." It is:

> Balance is the unique point where the seven-label DFT is also the orthonormal harmonic analysis induced by coordinate translation.

## 12. Interpretation and Limits

### What is now proved

1. The balance value is algebraically rigid throughout the entire odd-cycle family, not only for seven stations.
2. The framework's \(\mathbb Z_7\) is the \(m=3\) member of the general rule \(\mathbb Z_{2m+1}\).
3. Balance uniquely makes the visible station set equal the complete group it generates.
4. Rational imbalance creates a larger finite supercycle.
5. Irrational imbalance creates quasiperiodic dense closure.
6. Exact closure is discontinuous, while geometric and Fourier defects approach zero continuously.
7. Off balance, coordinate harmonic analysis deforms from an orthonormal DFT into an invertible nonorthogonal Vandermonde transform.

### What is not proved

1. That any physical, biological, psychological, or conscious system instantiates this station family.
2. That measured imbalance must be rational, irrational, or dynamically driven toward \(1/2\).
3. That a larger generated cycle is pathological, incoherent, or empirically undesirable.
4. That the number of spatial dimensions must be \(m=3\).
5. That this elementary theorem is new to mathematics as an abstract result.
