## Finding 4: the framework has formalized itself twice, and the isomorphism question applies reflexively

𝓛 = G ∘ R ∘ U_T (February, single scale, with recursion explicitly marked "optional" in its Axiom 4) and T = κ ∘ F (April onward, four beats plus nesting). The isomorphism program's strongest evidence now comes from the axiom it deferred.

**Pre-registered experiment (run only after countersign; stated now so the result cannot be dressed afterward).** Question: are Skel(𝓛) and Skel(F) related by the February doc's own morphism notion? Method: represent 𝓛 on ℂ⁴ with role-anchored matrices (G a diagonal gate on the boundary coordinate, U_T a transport unitary, R a convergence map onto the center coordinate; three representation choices swept); test existence of an invertible intertwiner f with f ∘ 𝓛 = F ∘ f by solving the Sylvester-type linear system for vec(f); spectrum equality checked first as the cheap necessary condition. Null: random loops at matched spectral radius, reporting how often intertwiners exist by chance. Honest expectation, stated in advance: exact conjugacy is unlikely (F is unitary; a loop with a real gate typically is not), so the informative result is the structure of the failure: which relaxation holds (homomorphism; conjugacy of unitary parts; conjugacy after removing the gate). Any of the four outcomes is a finding; only a post-hoc redefinition of success is not.

**A catch worth its own decision: the corpus now holds FOUR pathology taxonomies.** The two Lies (claims); the Wholeness Plane corners (bond states: Inflation, Severance, anomie); the February doc's five operator degenerations (mechanisms: non-selective gate, dead transport, non-receiving center, sealed return, absorptive center); and the charter's column confusions (frame errors: every named disorder as a confusion of the three columns). ## Finding 5: typing discipline for "maps to" rows (proposed editorial law)

The corpus uses one word across claims of at least four strengths. Proposed types, built from the February doc's own §6 vocabulary plus the corpus's grading practice:

- **Type 0, signature**: the row IS the abstract structure, not an instance of it (the master table's Form and Oneness rows; the ten stations). Nothing to verify; it defines what the others instantiate.
- **Type I, proven conjugacy**: explicit map computed and intertwining verified (the organ-pipe and laser-cavity worked example; the E / 𝒫 / m row, where the power equation is the map). Full theorem transfer licensed.
- **Type II, instantiation**: the target provably satisfies the axioms (bounded field, center, closing boundary), so skeleton theorems apply, but no explicit cross-domain map is computed (most of §2.12's rows; Schumann; the tense row against the engine). Transfer licensed for skeleton-derivable properties only.
- **Type III, witnessed convergence**: independent traditions or descriptions exhibiting the same shape (Ch33's rows; the trinity row, already marked candidate). Evidence FOR the claim, not an application of it; no transfer licensed.
- **Type IV, decoration**: resemblance noted, claimed as nothing (the corpus's standing noted-not-claimed device).

The guard against accommodation stays what it already is in practice: a row earns Type II by verifying axioms in the target domain, Type I by computing the map, and Type III survives only against nulls (the ℤ₇ DFT permutation nulls, the §27.7n matched-magnitude nulls, the pole-gap protocol's unfair-comparison principle). Forcing versus accommodating is the whole game; a structure loose enough to fit anything types everything at IV.
