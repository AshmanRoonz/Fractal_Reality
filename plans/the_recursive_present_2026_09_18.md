**The Recursive Present**

A synthesis of Ashman Roonz’s Circumpunct Framework, developed 17 September 2026 and extended with a recursion derivation on 18 September 2026.

The Circumpunct Framework proposes that reality is one by inclusion and many through limitation. “Energy is infinite. Power is finite” expresses the relationship between unactualized potential and particular expression. Energy, in this vocabulary, names metaphysical potential. Physical energy in joules and power in watts retain their usual meanings in the mathematical model below.

The statement \(E=1\) names the all-inclusive whole. Its oneness concerns inclusion: every particular thing belongs within reality. It does not assign the universe one joule or calculate a physical supply.

Within this ontology, the future has no actual events. It names possibility. Events become actual in the present and leave a past. “Every thing is a limitation on the infinite” means that an actual thing has a particular form, situation, and capacity. Its particularity gives it a way to participate. These are the framework’s proposed starting commitments.

Actualization is described as convergence through a point and emergence into finite expression. For human experience, the point names the indivisible point of view, called the soul. The mind is the field through which that center relates to the body and world. The body is the boundary through which private experience participates in shared reality. Their integration is centered wholeness.

The dimensional vocabulary distinguishes these organizing roles:

| Symbol | Role | Kind of oneness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(\infty\) | All-inclusive potential | Inclusion |
| \(0D\) | Point or center | Indivisibility of perspective; unity as a node at a specified scale |
| \(1D\) | Path or duration | Continuity |
| \(2D\) | Boundary or interface | Distinction |
| \(3D\) | Field | Coherence |
| \(4D\), or \(\odot\) | Centered whole | Integration |

Here, \(4D\) names centered wholeness within the framework. Establishing a correspondence between these roles and ordinary geometric dimensions or physical spacetime would require a further derivation.

The center’s unity can remain constant while its relationships change. Attention can orient toward a sensation, another person, a meaning, or an imagined possibility. Orientation can therefore concern responsiveness and felt attunement as well as spatial direction. A bodily sensation, including a feeling centered in the solar plexus, can anchor attention without being identified with the experiencing subject.

The aperture describes how the centered whole receives and expresses through its field and boundary. Its orientation concerns what it responds to. Its openness concerns the breadth and selectivity of exchange. Its bandwidth concerns how much can pass over time, once a measurable quantity has been specified. Its rhythm concerns how these properties vary. Narrow attention can sustain detailed engagement; broad attention can hold many things lightly. Receiving and expressing can occur together.

A geometric point has no width, so widening and narrowing belong to the relations and interface associated with the point. “Resonance” can describe felt responsiveness. A literal physical resonance would additionally require a defined oscillator, coupling, and measurable frequency. The experience motivates a description; the physical interpretation requires its own evidence.

The past shapes that aperture through what remains effective in the present. A body has a history. So do its skills, habits, memories, relationships, and surroundings. Earlier events leave structures that influence what can arrive and what responses are available.

**The boundary is history made active in the present.**

This formulation preserves the distinction between an event that has passed and the structure that now carries some of its consequences. The boundary retains selected traces, changes through ongoing interaction, and participates in current conditions. A learned skill opens possibilities; a settled habit makes some responses easier than others. Formation supplies both capacities and constraints.

An encounter can alter the field and boundary, shaping the aperture through which the next encounter occurs. This is the temporal part of the proposal: what happens changes the conditions of what can happen next.

Recursion adds a second relationship. **A centered whole at one scale participates as a point within the next scale.** The whole retains its internal multiplicity while functioning as one unit among other units. In the framework’s notation,

\[
\boxed{4D_n\xrightarrow{\text{recursion}}0D_{n+1}.}
\]

The index \(n\) identifies scale. The same entity can be a whole with parts at one scale and one participant at another, at the same time. Thus, \(0D=4D\) expresses an identification across scales, with the whole taking the role of a point at the next level.

This gives “everything is one and many” a recursive structure: many within, one among others. A collection of such units can participate in a further organized whole, which can itself become a unit at another scale. The conditions under which a collection constitutes a centered whole remain something the framework must specify.

A scale-relative point can carry substantial internal structure. The indivisibility claimed for a first-person perspective is a separate property. Representing a whole as one node does not by itself establish that the larger organization has a first-person perspective. A mathematical account of integration and an account of experience must explain their connection.

A compact representation of a centered whole is

\[
W_n(t)=\bigl(C_n(t),F_n(t),B_n(t),\mathcal L_n(t)\bigr),
\]

where \(C\) is the center, \(F\) the field, \(B\) the boundary, and \(\mathcal L\) their relationships. A scale map then gives

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_n[W_n(t)]=p_{n+1}(t).}
\]

Here \(p_{n+1}\) is the unit representing this whole in the next-scale description. It can carry retained properties and an effective state. To turn this representation into a predictive model, \(\mathcal R_n\) must preserve enough information to predict the larger-scale interactions being claimed. If omitted internal differences change those predictions, the higher-scale state needs additional variables, memory, or a stated approximation error.

Time and scale therefore need separate operations. For time, let \(e_k\) denote an actual event recorded after an interaction interval, and let \(H_k\) be the earlier record:

\[
H_{k+1}=H_k\Vert e_k.
\]

The symbol \(\Vert\) means append. This is ideal bookkeeping; a physical record has finite storage and an encoding. A schematic account of how history shapes the aperture is

\[
S_k=(F_k,B_k),\qquad
\kappa_k=\mathcal K(S_k),\qquad
S_{k+1}=\mathcal G(S_k,e_k,\xi_k).
\]

Here \(\kappa_k\) describes coupling, and \(\xi_k\) represents additional environmental influences during the interval. The maps \(\mathcal K\) and \(\mathcal G\) are functions still to be specified. These expressions locate the explanatory task: explain how present organization shapes exchange, and how exchange changes that organization. They do not yet predict attention, learning, or subjective experience.

The earlier flow model supplies one concrete mathematical example of bounded convergence and finite output. Choose a dimensionless state and coupling profile satisfying

\[
\Phi(t)\in\ell^2,\qquad \|\Phi(t)\|=1,\qquad
\|\kappa(t)\|\leq1.
\]

Then define

\[
\boxed{c(t)=\langle\kappa(t),\Phi(t)\rangle,\qquad |c(t)|\leq1.}
\]

An infinite-dimensional representation can yield a bounded local amplitude. The bound follows from the chosen normalization and coupling constraint. Infinite dimensionality does not establish infinite physical energy, and a singular point does not require a single scalar channel. The scalar overlap is one modeling choice.

Supply and response can be included through

\[
u(t)=q(t)c(t),\qquad 0\leq q(t)\leq1,
\qquad \boxed{\tau\dot z+z=u},
\]

with constant response time \(\tau>0\), initial condition \(|z(0)|\leq1\), and constant power scale \(P_*>0\). Here \(q\) describes supply availability and \(z\) is the local response amplitude. The physical completion is

\[
\begin{aligned}
P_{\rm in}&=P_*|u|^2, & P_{\rm out}&=P_*|z|^2,\\
P_{\rm back}&=P_*|u-z|^2, & U&=P_*\tau|z|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

The three powers are measured in watts, and stored energy \(U\) is measured in joules. The response law gives

\[
\boxed{\dot U=P_{\rm in}-P_{\rm out}-P_{\rm back},
\qquad 0\leq P_{\rm out}\leq P_*.}
\]

For example, differentiating \(U\) gives \(\dot U=2P_*\operatorname{Re}(\bar z u)-2P_*|z|^2\); expanding \(|u-z|^2\) gives the same balance. This is an exact identity within the selected model. Accounting for stored energy and transfers is the physical conservation requirement. [Feynman Lectures, Field Energy and Field Momentum](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_27.html)

Let \(R(t)\) be the upstream supply’s stored physical energy. With returned power fully recovered, negligible return delay, and no external replenishment,

\[
\dot R=-P_{\rm in}+P_{\rm back},
\qquad
\boxed{\int_0^T P_{\rm out}(t)\,dt\leq R(0)+U(0).}
\]

The inequality assumes \(R(T),U(T)\geq0\); supply availability must respect depletion. A finite initial supply supports a finite accumulated output. An indefinitely sustained positive output requires continuing replenishment. Metaphysical potential supplies no numerical physical power budget by itself.

These equations admit an optical interpretation: a resonantly driven cavity with two equally coupled ports, negligible internal loss, and an isolated mode in the slowly varying envelope regime. For a port energy-leakage rate \(1/\tau\), the substitutions \(a=\sqrt{P_*\tau}\,z\) and \(s=\sqrt{P_*}\,u\) recover the normalized response and input-output account. This correspondence is our inference from standard coupled-mode equations; it demonstrates a physical application of the mathematical form. [Kristensen et al., On the theory of coupled modes in optical cavity-waveguide structures](https://arxiv.org/html/1701.02929v1)

The optical example does not identify a cavity with a soul. Likewise, allowing \(\kappa(t)\) to vary does not by itself derive conscious attention or autonomous oscillation. Any controller or mechanism that changes coupling needs its own dynamics and physical accounting when it exchanges energy.

The earlier optional phase extension describes an additional reactive interaction under specified optical assumptions. Its effect must obey linked phase and transmission constraints once its coefficient is fixed. No nonzero effect or experimental bound has been measured in this project. It remains a conditional physical model, with no distinct prediction yet forced by the metaphysical interpretation.

**A first precise constraint on recursion is preservation of response.** If two internal states are assigned the same next-scale state, they must give the same predicted next-scale evolution under the same allowed external input. Otherwise the representation has discarded something the larger-scale prediction still needs.

Let \(\mathcal T^u_{n,t}\) describe evolution at scale \(n\), starting from a specified initial time and driven by an externally specified input history \(u\). Exact reduction to a state that evolves from its current value requires

\[
\boxed{\mathcal R_n\circ\mathcal T^u_{n,t}
=\mathcal T^u_{n+1,t}\circ\mathcal R_n.}
\]

Evolving the detailed whole and then reducing it must agree with reducing it first and evolving the retained state. The observable outputs we claim to predict must also be functions of that retained state and the specified input. This is a criterion for an exact representation; it is not a consequence of calling an entity a whole. Approximate representations need a specified tolerance and range of conditions.

A two-part example makes the criterion concrete. Choose dimensionless internal responses \(x_1,x_2\), positive relaxation rates \(a_1,a_2\), nonnegative coupling rate \(k\), and a common forcing \(f(t)\). Rates and forcing have units of inverse seconds. Assume

\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot x_1&=-a_1x_1+k(x_2-x_1)+f(t),\\
\dot x_2&=-a_2x_2+k(x_1-x_2)+f(t).
\end{aligned}
\]

For this example, choose the average \(X=(x_1+x_2)/2\) as the only exposed output and the proposed next-scale state. Keep track of the internal difference \(d=(x_1-x_2)/2\). Define

\[
\bar a=\frac{a_1+a_2}{2},\qquad
\delta=\frac{a_1-a_2}{2},\qquad
\gamma=\bar a+2k.
\]

Adding and subtracting the two equations gives exactly

\[
\boxed{
\dot X=-\bar aX-\delta d+f(t),\qquad
\dot d=-\delta X-\gamma d.
}
\]

If the two relaxation rates agree, \(a_1=a_2=a\), then \(\delta=0\), and

\[
\dot X=-aX+f(t),\qquad
\dot d=-(a+2k)d.
\]

The average evolves independently of the internal difference. For the specified common input and average output, the two-part system is exactly represented by one response variable, even when its parts initially differ. With \(\tau=1/a\) and normalized drive \(u=f/a\), this gives \(\tau\dot X+X=u\), the same response form used earlier. The equality of rates and the choice of accessible input and output are essential assumptions. Additional outputs can require additional retained state.

If the rates differ, the average alone fails this criterion. Set \(a_1=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(a_2=3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), \(k=1\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\), and \(f=0\). Two preparations have the same proposed next-scale state but different immediate responses:

| Initial internal state \((x_1,x_2)\) | \(X(0)\) | \(\dot X(0)\), in \(\mathrm{s}^{-1}\) | \(X(0.2\,\mathrm{s})\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| \((1,0)\) | 0.5 | -0.5 | 0.396687 |
| \((0,1)\) | 0.5 | -1.5 | 0.285455 |

No deterministic rule using only the current \(X\) and current \(f\) can predict both preparations. One remedy is to let the next-scale point carry the vector state \((X,d)\). Its unity as a node is compatible with a state containing several variables.

A second remedy expresses the hidden difference through its history. Solving its linear equation gives

\[
d(t)=e^{-\gamma t}d(0)
-\delta\int_0^t e^{-\gamma(t-s)}X(s)\,ds.
\]

Substitution yields the exact memory equation

\[
\boxed{
\dot X(t)=-\bar aX(t)+f(t)
-\delta e^{-\gamma t}d(0)
+\delta^2\int_0^t e^{-\gamma(t-s)}X(s)\,ds.
}
\]

The memory kernel is \(K(t-s)=\delta^2e^{-\gamma(t-s)}\). Its strength vanishes when the rates agree; its decay time is \(1/\gamma\). The explicit initial-state term also matters. A memory integral alone does not eliminate dependence on the omitted initial difference.

This provides a precise mathematical interpretation of part of the proposal: **when internal structure is omitted from a whole’s next-scale description, its influence can appear as memory in the point’s response.** In the detailed model, that influence is carried by a present internal state. This simple derivation is an instance of the memory terms obtained when unresolved variables are eliminated in the Mori–Zwanzig approach. [Wang, Benner, and Heiland, Partial Observation of Linear Systems with the Mori–Zwanzig Formalism](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23341)

The result concerns effective response memory. It does not yet derive a changing aperture profile, learning, autonomous oscillation, or first-person experience. It also does not establish that the past literally occupies a boundary. It shows how the effects of internal history can remain relevant when a many-part system is treated as one unit.

For verification, exact matrix-exponential solutions give the two numerical outputs in the table. Independent numerical integration of the memory convolution agreed with their derivatives within \(2.0\times10^{-15}\) at the checked times. Four rational-parameter comparisons of the detailed and reduced zero-initial-state transfer expressions agreed exactly. These are mathematical checks of a chosen example, with no new experiment or empirical claim.

The philosophical claim about ontology remains intact: an accurate account of what exists, translated faithfully into mathematics and developed by valid reasoning, should describe reality within its stated scope. It could recover established physics. Distinct predictions become necessary when empirically distinguishing accounts that otherwise predict the same observations.

The present work provides a proposed ontology, a recursive organization of its concepts, and one bounded, conserving physical model. Its central open tasks are to derive the coupling and scale maps, explain the conditions for centered integration, and connect those descriptions to measurable systems and first-person experience. Normalization, the square-amplitude law, and the response equation are additional modeling assumptions; their mathematical consistency does not establish the ontology.

**The present is centered wholeness. The past persists in the structures shaping its boundary and aperture. Possibilities become particular events through its participation, and those events leave further history. As one whole, it participates as a point within a greater whole.**
