# Witnesses for Time: Who Else Conceives It This Way

**Status: session record, 2026-08-19, drafted in answer to Ashman's question ("Does anyone else in the world conceive of time this way?"); pending countersign as the witnesses apparatus for §4.11 and §4.11a.** Discipline: cite, not rediscover; every neighbor named at honest distance; the residual claimed precisely. ## The direct answer

As a whole: no. The full conception (possibility converging through an indivisible present into settled constraint, PLUS time identified with the scale axis, PLUS the tense-channel kinematics on the pump's conjugate phases, PLUS the E, 𝒫, m economic triple with conservation as the composite, PLUS the diagnostics) is not held by anyone else that the record of philosophy and physics shows. In pieces: yes, and the pieces have distinguished company. The mosaic and its arithmetic are the framework's; several tiles have famous prior owners who must become citations.

## The nearest single neighbor: Whitehead (must be cited going forward)

Process and Reality (1929) is the closest thing in the world to §4.11a. Concrescence: many potentials converge into one atomic "actual occasion" of experience; on completion the occasion perishes into "objective immortality," becoming settled data that every future occasion must inherit ("the many become one, and are increased by one"). Map it: potentials converging = possibility supplied by the open; the atomic occasion = the indivisible present (Whitehead's epochal theory of becoming makes the unit of process indivisible, explicitly not a duration); perishing into data = the past as deposited constraint; prehension = inheritance; experience all the way down = the panexperientialist cost the essays already accept openly. Differences, equally explicit: Whitehead has no scale-axis identification, no octave or lattice, no quantitative layer, no falsification handles; his God and creativity are structured differently from ∞. Verdict: the framework's temporal engine is Whiteheadian in silhouette and proceeds where Whitehead stopped: into arithmetic and diagnostics.

## The ancient anchors

**Aristotle**: potentiality and actuality (dynamis and energeia) are the original vocabulary of "possibility becomes actuality," and Physics IV holds the other tile verbatim-adjacent: the now is not a part of time but a partless limit, the indivisible link between past and future. **Augustine** (Confessions XI): the present "flies with such speed" that it has no extension; only an indivisible point may be called present; and his threefold present (memory, attention, expectation) is the soul-side triple of record, conversion, and possibility: the calibration loop's ancestor.

## The physics-side neighbors

**The growing block** (C. D. Broad, 1923): past real and settled, future unreal, the present as the edge where reality accrues; the framework's difference is that the future is real AS possibility, not nothing. **George Ellis's evolving (crystallizing) block**: the present as where quantum indeterminacy becomes determinate: potentiality converting to actuality at the moving edge; the closest physics cousin of E → 𝒫 → m. **Heisenberg** revived Aristotle's potentia explicitly for the quantum state ("a quantitative version of the old concept of potentia"), converting to actuality at measurement. **Causal set theory** (Sorkin's sequential growth): becoming as the asynchronous birth of elements, with no global time order: the physics cousin of "infinitely many presents, one per center, no universal now." **Prigogine** (From Being to Becoming): irreversibility and becoming as fundamental rather than illusory. Relativity itself supplies the two-door concordance already pinned (per-event presents pointlike; no shared extended now).

## The existential and clinical neighbors

**Heidegger**: ecstatic temporality: the future as primary (being-ahead-of-itself), thrownness as the inherited (the past as constraint one starts from), the moment of resolute presence: the existential register of the countersigned mechanics. **Minkowski** (Le Temps Vecu, 1933) and **Thomas Fuchs** (phenomenological psychiatry): lived time as the clinical axis: depression as the blocked or closed future, mania as flight into the forward, melancholia as dominance of the past. The charter's tense pathologies have this lineage and should cite it; the narcissism refinement (past-terminal convergence on the self's image) appears native. **Buddhist momentariness** (ksanavada): reality as momentary arisings, each indivisible: the contemplative cousin of the atomic present.

## The residual: what appears to be the framework's alone

1. **Time as scale**: above in scale is ahead in time; your parts are your past; the greater whole is your future. No established position identifies the temporal axis with the nesting axis (the faint physics echo, radial direction as energy scale in holography, is not this claim).
2. **The tense-channel kinematics**: the future arriving by convergence and the past made by emergence, as the conjugate phases of one pump, with the silent center between the two sentences.
3. **The economic triple**: E, 𝒫, m as future, present, past, with the power equation as the conversion and conservation as the statement that the composite arrow is the identity.
4. **The two doors**: the future at the aperture (direct, unblocked), the past at the boundary (perception lags); the radial geometry of the tenses.
5. **The temporal balance**: ◐ as the future-channel share; health as the full breath; the corner-and-middle two-layer rule.
6. **The binding**: no ancestor ties the picture to an operator with theorems, a lattice with constants, and named kill conditions. The ancestors offered pictures; the framework offers a picture that can lose.
