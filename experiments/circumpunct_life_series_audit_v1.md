## 3. What the series is

Nineteen agent-based ecologies on small toroidal lattices (36 to 144 sites). Agents hold energy, pay metabolism, maintain a shared habitat or infrastructure at a cost, and (from v0.2) reproduce and die. Each version asks one question, compares a learner with matched controls on held-out seeds, and reports paired differences. The roadmap:

| Releases | Question |
|---|---|
| v0.1 to v0.4 | one persistent world with learned gates, inheritance, cultural transfer, social links |
| v0.5 to v0.9 | can a collective centre itself: elected coordinator, flow locus, distributed regulation, warnings, influence hotspot |
| v1.0 to v1.2 | costly institutions: relays, protective medium, protocol voting |
| v1.3 to v1.5 | self-formed group boundaries, relation learning, controlled on/off trials |
| v1.6 to v1.9 | self-initiated experiments, value of information, learned cost and delayed value of knowing |

The circumpunct roles (center, path, boundary, field, whole) are used in every report as engineering labels, and every report says so.

Three patterns, each of which the authors state for single versions and none of which they state for the series:

1. **A fixed or simpler policy equals or beats the learner in 17 of 19 versions.** The exceptions are v0.6 (use-dependent link strengthening beats every fixed-link control) and v1.5 (on/off crossover trials beat always-protect by 3.2, but not the hand-designed risk rule). In v1.0 a random investor at the learner's own rate beats the learner; in v1.2 and v1.8 freezing the learner beats letting it keep learning.
2. **The agent's own action-outcome feedback carries information; what others say mostly does not.** Shuffling the agent's own feedback bites in 10 of 13 cases. Displacing other agents' messages bites in 1 of 4, and in two (v0.7, v1.0) the displaced message does better than the true one. Where it does not bite (v1.1, v1.2, v1.9), the authors' explanation is right: all agents share the regime, so a scrambled signal keeps the regime's timing. This is the same thing Experiment 13 found for its shuffle control: it bites in a fast world (.29) and not in a slow one (.54), because a shuffled signal keeps whatever is shared across the shuffle.
3. **Removing the measured "centre" never costs more than removing a random part.** Seven versions test it (v0.5 elected node, v0.6 flow locus, v0.7 and v0.9 influence hotspots, v1.0 relay sites, v1.3 to v1.5 high-degree links); none finds a cost beyond that of one part. In v0.5 the population after the elected centre is removed (32.5 over the next 25 steps) equals the local-only population with nothing removed (32.7). In v0.9 killing the eight most influential agents raises the population (93.7 against 91.0 for random, 90.9 for none); the authors note the rejuvenation effect of vacancies.

## 6. What the reports do not say

- v0.7: the misplaced-warning population is also far more robust to injury. Removing six sites costs the distributed field 12 agents (80.7 to 68.8) and the misplaced-warning field 1.6 (98.1 to 96.4). The injury table in the report shows only the distributed field. Over the whole run the misplaced field also maintains more (0.383 against 0.350 per step), which is the report's own unquantified hypothesis for why it wins.
- v0.8: the gated, naive and shuffled warning fields all equal the local learner within 0.3 agents because the learned trust goes negative and the messages are weighted to nothing; the communication arms are a no-op, which the report says in words but the table makes stark (72.39, 72.39, 72.16, 72.39).
- v0.9: the hazard-informed "oracle" (21.1) and the adaptive-trust learner (17.9) both collapse below the scrambled-message control (79.5). The report calls the oracle "not a performance upper bound" and the adaptive learner a failure; it does not say that knowing the future hazard three steps ahead is worse than a misplaced warning, which means the forecast is not what the maintenance rule needs.
- v1.1: resetting newborns' learned policy (98.1) does as well as inheriting it (97.9); cultural inheritance adds nothing here, as in v0.4 (+0.14 points) and v1.3 (private learning 54.7 against 55.0 with gossip). The series tests inheritance of acquired policy four times and never finds a gain.
- v1.5 and v1.6: the learner's advantage over "always protect" comes with half the links (47 against 100), so it is an efficiency gain; its survival gain over the no-trial policy in v1.6 is negative (−0.9).
- Across the series, the only measured quantity that persists through complete turnover of the agents is stored infrastructure (relay strength, protective medium, reserves), and in each case the report says it persists "by construction".

## 7. Bearing on the Theory of Wholeness and on Experiments 12 and 13

The theory distinguishes a list (the mean of the parts, which acts as a point until it is cut), a node (a part the other parts read: chair at low gain, leader at high gain) and a ring with a 0 (a closed turn of phase among the parts, forced, which no part occupies and no cut removes without breaking the turn). Experiments 12 and 13 built all three from the same parts and found that the 0 buys persistence and corrigibility, not the decision: the list decides as well as the ring; the leader commits once and never moves.

Every centre in the Life series is a list or a node:

- v0.5's elected coordinator is a node: a part chosen by local endorsement, whose broadcast is the mean of its neighbours' reported needs. The series finds what Experiment 12 found for the chair: it decides no better than the parts alone, and removing it costs one part.
- v0.6's flow locus, v0.7's throughput locus, v0.9's influence hotspot, v1.0's influential relays and v1.3 to v1.5's high-degree links are measurements over a list (an argmax of a field computed by the experimenter). Cutting the argmax of a list costs one element, which is what seven versions measure.
- No version builds a ring with a 0. The fields the series uses (stress, warning, message) are diffusive and couple the common mode, which Experiment 12 showed ends the turn rather than making one. The series' negative verdict, "no indispensable centre emerged", is therefore a verdict on hubs and elected nodes. It agrees with the theory and does not test the theory's centre, because that centre was never built.

Two further points of contact:

- Persistence. The series finds persistence through turnover only in stored quantities (a medium that outlasts its occupants). The theory's persistence is a relation among the parts that outlasts any part (the turn). Both outlast the parts; only the second is a centre in the theory's sense. A test that distinguishes them would remove the stored quantity and keep the relation, which no version does.
- Deciding. The series' author proposed, on 2026-11-06, an organism with "an individual decision-making center". The series' own v0.5 had already run that design in the elected form and found no benefit, which is also what Experiments 12 and 13 found for nodes: the decision does not need a centre; what a centre of the right type adds is holding.

Nothing in the series takes a framework input, and nothing in it bears on the physics. It does strengthen one line of the theory's guard: a measured hub is prominence, not convergence.
