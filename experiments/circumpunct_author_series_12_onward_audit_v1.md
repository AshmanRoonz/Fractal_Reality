## 3. What the READMEs do not say

The READMEs are careful: each names its engineered grammar, its researcher-chosen probes, its failed prototypes (17's corner probes, 18's and the series' earlier estimator), its extrapolation failures and its unequal data budgets. The additions are about where the gains sit.

- **14.** The product x·y is itself rank one, so a rank-one SVD of its grid recovers it by construction (singular energy .9996); the learned operator takes 99.9% of the headroom from the additive model to the oracle for x·y, 97% for tanh·tanh, 72% for sin(2xy) and 30% for |x − y|, where it is worse than the additive model in 14% of contexts. Detection is a 2 by 2 interaction test at about 120 standard errors against a threshold of 4 (additive worlds sit at 1.2); it is not a close call, and the noise table shows where it would be.
- **16.** The adaptive selector's gain over the fixed quadratic-with-lag model comes from three of the eight families (oscillation +.31, saturation +.21, piecewise +.12, dynamic saturation +.13); on the other four the two are the same model or within .002. The adaptive choice beats the correct-family template in 8 of 128 holdout trials and ties it in 114: a wrong family can predict better than the true one fitted from a local minimum, which is the README's own warning that low error is not identification. Under extrapolation the template (.125) beats the selector (.146).
- **17.** Random probes already recover three of the four families at 92 to 100%; the whole active gain lives in product squared (83% against 29%), the family whose probe design was repaired after the prototype failure, and the one where identification matters least (additive .213, reuse .166, oracle .094, because (xy)² is small in the domain). Active beats random in 16 seeds where random failed and never the reverse; on transfer error the paired difference is +.010 with an interval that includes zero. When the macro is wrong (38 of 288 runs) reuse (.309) is worse than searching from scratch (.286); when right, reuse equals the oracle exactly, since both fit the same four coefficients.
- **19.** On the same 192 holdout problems the active policy is right where the fixed extremes are wrong in 11 cases and the reverse in 8, a net of 3; its out-of-range error is lower in 93 of 192, a coin toss; and its first two probes are the fixed schedule's own ends of the range (3.6 and .35), so the adaptive part is the two middle probes. Broad random's mean error (.026) is 3.4 times its median; the README reports medians. Every wrong choice of the active learner is the flexible cubic, never a wrong parametric family.
- **20.** Directed against fixed probes on the same 175 problems: 4 to 1. The expanded grammar's gain is confined to the two composite families (hybrid .119 to .005, broken .101 to .012); on power and exponential laws it equals the single law and over-expands in 7% and 4% of main cases. Without the falsification step a composite is promoted in 100% of composite cases, so the step costs nothing where it is right, and in 21 to 31% of simple ones. Under noise the first thing lost is abstention: at sd .05 the unlisted bump is recognised as unknown in 46% and the hybrid in 64%, while power and exponential stay at 98%. Far extrapolation (x 3.15 to 4.5) doubles every in-range error.
- **21.** Active against the fixed X/Y schedule: 5 and 6 more correct of 360, one fewer intervention (3.62 against 4.63), the same number confidently wrong (6 against 6 and 6 against 9). Three interventions suffice because a single do(X = 2) carries a likelihood ratio near 32 to 1, and the active rule never observes, since observation has zero information gain by construction. The mixed world outside the library is read with a posterior above .95 in 56% of cases and flagged in 19%: the learner is confidently sure of a wrong model four times in five, which the README says. The sensor-noise table cannot be produced by the shipped script, whose main run uses one noise level and never writes that file.
- **22.** The "program synthesis" is the choice of a larger clamp: information per cost rises from .16 at one pulse to .27 at two and .32 at three, over a catalogue of 19 programs enumerated before any data. Active against fixed: 1 more exact of 165 on the holdout and 1 fewer on the main set, with .59 of cost saved. The "truthful report of an ambiguous class" is not learned: the equivalence classes are computed analytically from the program set and all three likelihoods at the start of every episode, and the declaration is the class whose mass passes .95, so the passive interface reports non-identifiability at 0 steps and the X-only interface reports the B/C class in 66.7% because the table says so. The mixed world is read with confidence .90 on average and flagged in 12.5%.
- **23.** The adaptive controller's gain over always building the Y actuator is the build cost it did not pay: net score +.049 and +.019 against always-Y, of which +.046 and +.047 is saved cost, with accuracy +.003 on the main set and −.028 (10 of 360) on the holdout. Every one of its 380 builds is the gain-2 Y actuator; the "inexpensive X precision" the README allows for was never chosen. The build decision is one exact value-of-information calculation at step 4 over 8 programs with known response means, so building in the B/C worlds and not in A follows from the class table; the 24 B/C episodes in which it did not build were right half the time, the coin toss the table predicts. Never-upgrade's 66% is two worlds right and the third never (the forced tie-break picks B over C); its honest structural report names the pair, and only the forced guess scores. With the noise doubled the build rate falls to .56 and accuracy to .80, because the posterior after four X probes is less decided.
- **18.** The learned operator sits .010 above the oracle on the holdout: a 39-triangle spline fitted on 565 calibration points reproduces any smooth one-variable curve, so the gap to the scratch fit from 12 points is the data budget, which the README names. Promotion margins are wide (minimum validation gain .203 against the .025 threshold); the three false promotions in the unrelated-rules control had gains of .052 to .073 against a maximum of .050 among the rejected, so the threshold sits just under the noise of that control.

## 4. Bearing on the Theory of Wholeness

With Experiment 12 the author's series leaves attention and moves to the field: what the interior can hold and how it grows its vocabulary. In their own tables the center is "a persistent evaluation reference", the line is the choice of what to test, and Φ is "the expandable predictive representation". Every method is a known statistical one (interaction tests, SVD, model selection by validation, symbolic regression with active learning, spline bases), stated as such, and every result is of the form: given a grammar that contains the truth or something near it, validation finds it; outside the grammar it fails or abstains. Nothing in 12 to 23 concerns a whole, a center in canon's sense, a turn or a clock, and nothing tests canon's placement of attention. The series' pattern from the Life releases persists in a weaker form: where the function lies in the class, the oracle or true template is within .001 to .01 of the learner, and the large margins are against fixed additive models, probes kept near the starting data, or passive observation of a world built so that observation cannot tell. In 19 to 22 the author says it: a fixed, well-chosen set of probes is nearly as good as the adaptive one (net 3 of 192, 4 to 1 of 175, 5 or 6 of 360, 1 of 165). What 21 and 22 add that the theory can use is the boundary result: with an X-only interface two of three worlds are provably indistinguishable, and with passive observation all three, so what the interface permits sets what any attention can learn. That is canon's ○ as filter stated as a theorem in one toy family, and 22's own README calls it the crisp interaction between attention and boundary. Experiment 23 adds the other half: the boundary can be changed, at a price, and a controller that knows the price and the value of information changes it exactly when the class table says the old boundary cannot answer. In the theory's terms that is the boundary learned as a gate (the open item in the doc's next-test decision) in its simplest form, chosen once from a catalogue rather than learned.

What the series does show, and the theory does not yet have, is a model of the field's content changing its own vocabulary: a list of hypotheses that lengthens (11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 18) or a choice among forms (16). In the theory the field mediates relation; these experiments are about what it mediates with. If the Theory of Wholeness is to say anything about imagination (canon's Φ as "the space of experience and imagination"), this is the register in which it would be tested, and the author's series is now a usable baseline for it.

## 4a. The integrated agent v0.1 ("Experiment 24"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v01_integrated_learning.zip`: one Python object, `CircumpunctAgent`, with a `Center` (a value function, the minimum of two resources, and two weights), an `Attention` (an argmax over need-weighted effect estimates with an exploration bonus), a `Boundary` (flags for a spectrometer, known modes and actuators) and a `Memory` (an event list and a concept dictionary), and three task modules run one after another: a resonance world (two channels whose effects swap at step 70 and a third channel from step 115, announced by an ambient observable), a symbolic world (the Experiment 17 grammar and probe engine imported as is) and a causal world (the three Gaussian worlds of 21 to 23 with a Y actuator built by rule). The capability matrix is honest: 6 of the 23 prior capacities are LIVE, 7 PARTIAL, 10 REFERENCE only, and the README says the three domains "have not been fused into a single universal learner", which is its own fifth milestone. Check script `agent01_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** `summary.json` equals the validation log and recomputes from the three trial tables (480, 288 and 288 rows) to the last digit; every README figure matches: resonance .8230 against .1100 fixed and .0019 for the learner without the upgrade; transfer RMSE .0677 against .7608 without the macro and .0989 with random probes; structure 96 of 96 against 84; causal 96 of 96 against 64 without the Y actuator and 94 with nonadaptive tests.

**What the README does not say.** The spectrometer is built by a rule, not a decision: the code builds when the ambient observable exceeds .9, and the world sets it to 1.25 at step 115, so every seed builds at step 115 and finds the third mode, which pays .064 to both resources against a decay of .041, so once found the task is over (final weakest resource 1.000 in every seed). The learner without the upgrade is below the fixed two-channel controller in 95 of 96 seeds (.002 against .110): learning alone loses to alternation here, as in the Life series. Curiosity and correction change the outcome by .0000 and −.0002, which the README states. The Y actuator is built by a threshold rule (posterior on X-causes-Y below .12 with three tests left), not by the value-of-information calculation of 23, and it fires in 100% of the two ambiguous worlds and 9% of the identifiable one; without it the 64 of 96 is the class table. The symbolic result is Experiment 17 again, since the module imports it: active probes beat random ones only in the 12 seeds where random probes missed the motif (product squared .58 against 1.00) and tie elsewhere. What the three tasks share is a memory list, a boundary flag set and one random stream; no task reads another's state.

**The legacy folder.** `legacy/source/experiment_01.py` to `_23.py` are described as copies of the originals. Nineteen match archives received here byte for byte, with the author's numbering offset by one around 5 and 6: their `_05` is the resonant-attention study sent as "Experiment 4", their `_06` is an "Experiment 5" (intrinsically prioritised resonant attention, stores and a softmax budget) never sent, and the learned-resonance study sent as "Experiment 6" is absent from the folder although the matrix lists it as LIVE. `_01` to `_04` are spatial studies never sent: attractors in a closed 3-ball, Cahn-Hilliard phase separation on a 3-torus, a chemoattractant centre-relocation model, and a conservative surface-field model of directional attention. They are marked REFERENCE and are not run by the agent.

**Against the one-system design discussed the same day.** This is the router version: one object, three worlds in sequence, a shared log. The design discussed with Ashman is the nested version: one world that defeats each capacity alone, the capacities as sub-wholes with their own centres joined at contact, a whole that reads each as a point and attunes among their rhythms, and the list of the same learners as the control. The author's v0.1 does not have a whole in that sense (nothing holds, nothing turns, nothing nests, no part reads another), and its own negative finding is the Life pattern once more: the one thing that mattered was the interface change, triggered by a designed observable. Its honest matrix and milestone list make it a usable starting inventory for the rebuild, not the rebuild.

## 4b. The integrated agent v0.2 ("Experiment 25"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v02_experiment_25.zip` keeps every v0.1 file unchanged (only the README and capability matrix differ; all v0.1 results and legacy sources are byte-identical) and adds `circumpunct/continuous.py`: one world of 260 steps in which every action, resonating on a channel, building a scanner, probing the new channel's response surface, probing or repairing a causal fault, building a Y actuator, takes the same single time step. Hidden events are jittered by seed: a channel swap at steps 69 to 90, a third channel from steps 113 to 138 announced by an anomaly flag, a fault from steps 158 to 185 announced by an alarm that stays on until the right repair. The controller is a hand-written decision ladder (`decide()`): maintenance first when the weaker resource is under .24, build the scanner when the anomaly flag is on, run the causal routine when the alarm is on (probe by information gain, build Y when the X-causes-Y posterior falls under .18, repair the most likely cause when the posterior passes .935, up to three tries), probe the field up to four times, revisit any channel unsampled for 12 steps, else resonate on the channel whose learned effects best match the need. The study write-up, release notes and matrix say all of this plainly: a partially integrated engineering prototype, a designed meta-scheduler, provided anomaly and fault signals, a four-candidate symbolic grammar and three given causal worlds; "references to all 23 earlier experiments, but not 23 fully active modules in one runtime". Every file matches its manifest hash (76 of 76). Check script `agent02_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** `summary.json` equals the validation stdout and recomputes, means and standard errors, from the 672 trial rows (96 worlds by 7 policies); the study's two tables match (final-80 weakest resource: integrated .632, no symbolic .590, no causal .316, no expansion .098, slow correction .214, no curiosity .083, fixed .036; repairs 96, 96, 0, 59, 96, 96, 0 of 96; symbolic matches 96, none, 96, none, 41, 20, none); 91 of 96 first repairs correct; the paired bootstrap intervals reproduce to .002 with a fresh bootstrap.

**What the write-up does not say, or says and should be read with.** The ablation ladder is a removal ladder, not a contribution ladder: without the scanner no later capacity can run, and the curiosity switch also turns off the scanner build in 79% of worlds and the symbolic fit in 79%, so the largest "effects" are prerequisites removed (the write-up names the curiosity confound). The one clean contribution is the symbolic module: +.042 in the last 80 steps for 4 research steps, and −.011 over the whole run, which the write-up also says. First-repair accuracy is 1.00, .92 and .90 by true cause, and eventual repair is guaranteed by the alarm and three tries. The symbolic match of 96 of 96 is a four-way choice among candidates that always contain the truth, with a median of 82 labelled examples by the end. The integrated controller spends 10.4 of 260 steps on research and the rest resonating; its primary outcome has a per-world spread of .18, with 22 of 96 worlds below .5. The fixed two-channel controller ends at 0.000 in every world because the decay beats alternation by construction, as in v0.1.

**Against the nested design.** This is a real step from v0.1: one world, one action slot, capacities that compete for time, hidden events the controller does not read. It is also exactly the control I named for the nested experiment: the same learners arranged as a list under one hand-written router, with no whole. Nothing in it holds a state through a blank, turns, attunes, or reads another part; the "center" is a scarcity threshold and the "attention" is the if-ladder's first true branch. So v0.2 is usable as the list condition, and its world is close to the one world I proposed, lacking only the two cues that compete by rhythm. What the nested version would add is the whole: sub-wholes with their own centres joined at contact, read by the whole as points, with a clock that attunes among their rhythms, and the prediction from the record that this decides no better and holds and changes better.

## 4c. The integrated agent v0.3 ("Experiment 26"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v03_experiment_26.zip` keeps v0.2 unchanged (the only edits to the continuous world are a faster leave-one-out computation and a public decay multiplier) and adds `circumpunct/meta.py`: five scheduler profiles that differ in thresholds (how much evidence before repairing, .82 to .985; how low a resource may fall before research stops, .10 to .44; how long before a stale channel is revisited, 6 to 25 steps; probe limits and exploration bonuses), four publicly observable regimes (low and high resource pressure, cheap and expensive inquiry), and a contextual bandit, a 4 by 5 table of training means with six forced episodes per cell and Thompson-style sampling, that picks one profile at the start of each episode. Training 480 episodes; held-out 128 world-regime pairs with every profile run on each; 96 shifted worlds. Check script `agent03_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** `summary.json` recomputes from the 1,152 held-out rows (means, standard errors, by regime) and the shifted summary from its 288 rows, to the last digit; the write-up's figures match: learned .7264, fixed causal-first .7235, balanced .6646, random .6674; the paired gain over balanced +.0618 [.0499, .0738], 9.3%; over the fixed winner +.0029; shifted .6941, .6383, .6922; 480 training episodes, 120 per context, six per cell at least.

**What the write-up says and should be read with.** The write-up is candid that the gain over the strongest fixed profile is .003 and that most cases are ties. The rows show the rest. The fixed training winner and the causal-first profile are the same rows, so "the strongest fixed policy" is the winner chosen on training data. Learned and causal-first tie in 96 of 128 pairs and differ only in the low-pressure regime (+.0115 there, 0 elsewhere), where the table prefers investigate-first by a margin of .021. A random profile (.6674) equals the balanced v0.2 schedule (.6646) within .003, so the whole gain over v0.2 is the preset causal-first, which the learner found and then improved by .003. The gain over balanced lives in the two hard regimes (+.103 and +.118 against +.020 and +.006), where causal-first repairs on less evidence and keeps researching at lower resources. Every controller repairs in 128 of 128 held-out worlds and the learned policy's first repair is right in 128 of 128; the differences are timing and cost, not success. The scheduler itself is 20 numbers.

**In the theory's terms.** The learned answer is the one knob again: act on less evidence when the world drains fast, explore more when it does not. That is Experiment 13's "persistence pays at the tempo the gain sets" and Experiment 10's hazard, found by a bandit over presets whose main difference is that threshold. It is also the fourth time in the author's series that the adaptive layer's gain over the best fixed rule is a few thousandths (19: net 3 of 192; 20: 4 to 1 of 175; 21: 6 of 360; here .003). As a whole, v0.3 is v0.2 with a dial on the router; nothing holds, turns, nests or reads another part, and the dial is set once per episode from outside the episode.

## 4d. The integrated agent v0.4 ("Experiment 27"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v04_experiment_27.zip` keeps v0.3 unchanged and adds `circumpunct/online_meta.py`: a world whose public regime switches once mid-episode (six transitions, switch time 91 to 179), and an executive that re-chooses one of the five v0.3 profiles every 12 steps or when a public signature changes, from a 7 by 5 table seeded with the v0.3 table and updated after each block by a short-window reward, the update clipped to ±.018 of the prior. Controls: the same switcher with updates off, a reactive v0.3 (re-choose from the public context, no learning), v0.3 fixed at the start, and the fixed causal-first profile. Training 180 episodes, held-out 96, 37 tests. The write-up states its central negative result: the no-feedback switcher produced identical decisions and the best fixed profile scored higher. Check script `agent04_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** `summary.json` recomputes from the 480 held-out rows (means, standard errors, by transition) to the last digit; the write-up's table and three paired intervals match (online .7717, no feedback .7717, reactive .7712, episode-fixed .7343, fixed causal-first .7733; +.0374 [.0196, .0551] over episode-fixed; +.00049 over reactive; −.00156 against fixed causal-first); the online and no-feedback rows are identical in every column; .77 switches and 24.09 updates per episode.

**What the write-up does not say.** The gain over the episode-fixed policy lives entirely in the two transitions that start easy and turn hard (+.171 and +.063), where a profile fixed at the start stays investigate-first; in the other four the five controllers are identical or within .01, because they start or end in a regime whose profile is already causal-first. The whole experiment is one switch, investigate-first to causal-first when pressure rises, and a reactive lookup makes it as well as the learner (tied in 79 of 96). The feedback could not have changed a decision: the update is clipped to ±.018 of the prior, and the prior's margins between the best and second profile are .021, .091 and .053 in three of the four base contexts, so only the cheap-inquiry context (margin .003) was ever in reach; after 180 training episodes the largest cell moved .018 and no argmax moved in any of the 7 contexts. The training file's switch and update columns are cumulative counters across episodes, not per-episode figures.

**In the theory's terms.** The ceiling here is the fixed causal-first profile, and the executive's only real move is to the tempo knob when the world's tempo changes, which the reactive lookup does from the public cue alone. Nothing in the world requires attending to a rhythm or holding anything through a blank, so nothing distinguishes a whole from a router here. Across v0.1 to v0.4 the honest trajectory is: the interface did the work (v0.1), one world with one slot (v0.2), a dial on the router (v0.3), the dial moved mid-episode by a cue (v0.4), with the adaptive layer's margin over the best fixed rule at .003 and then −.002.

## 4e. The integrated agent v0.5 ("Experiment 28"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v05_experiment_28.zip` keeps v0.4 unchanged (the README apart) and adds `circumpunct/hidden_meta.py`, `hidden_benchmark.py`, `hidden_demo.py` and a test file: a world whose public conditions never change while the payoff of the two resonance modes secretly does. Novelty, fault and swap are switched off. **In the theory's terms.** v0.5 is the first of the agents in which the context is read from outcomes rather than from a public cue, which is the ○-as-filter point of 21 to 23 turned inward: what the whole can know of the world's tempo is what its own gain readings let through. The result is E13's again: a stiff map is best at the tempo it was built for and loses to a fast learner when the tempo moves off it; the fast learner is distractible on the main set and corrigible on the stress set. The margin over the best fixed rule is within noise for the third time (.003, −.002, now +.001). What the executive detects is a change in the variability of its own gain, one scalar; nothing holds through a blank, nothing resonates, and the two "contexts" are two tempos of one knob.

## 4f. The integrated agent v0.6 ("Experiment 29"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v06_experiment_29.zip` keeps v0.5 unchanged (the README apart; the stray `online_meta.tmp` is gone) and adds `circumpunct/adaptive_rate.py` and a benchmark on the same hidden-volatility world. Four filters track each mode's net effect at rates .05, .18, .45 and .85; a softmax of their discounted squared errors mixes them; the fraction of recent readings that jumped by more than .032 (window 54) both pulls the mixture toward a fixed fast vector and sets how often the alternative mode is re-tested (every 4 to 29 steps). Controls: rate only, cadence only, three fixed settings (slow .05 and 25, medium .28 and 12, fast .85 and 5), the v0.5 detector and the v0.5 best fixed profile. 120 held-out worlds (SS, and SV, VS, VV at toggle periods 8, 14 and 24; 12 each), 36 stress worlds (periods 5 and 32, noise 1.7 times). The study states its own central result: no demonstrated superiority over fixed fast. Check script `agent06_check.py` and `agent06_extra.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** `summary.json` and `stress_summary.json` recompute from the 960 and 288 rows to the last digit (means, standard deviations, paired intervals, by scenario); the study's eight scores, three paired intervals, four scenario examples, cadence and rate figures and stress figures all match (adaptive .8847, fixed fast .8831, cadence only .8748, v0.5 detector .7866, rate only .7732, fixed medium .7007, fixed slow .4405, v0.5 best fixed .7835; +.0981 [.0607, .1354] over the v0.5 detector; +.0016 [−.0193, .0225] over fixed fast; +.0099 [.0022, .0176] over cadence only; stress .9208 against .9043, +.0166 [−.0208, .0539]).

**What the study does not say.** The +.098 over v0.5 is two scenarios: SV and VV at period 8, where the v0.5 map collapses (.309 and .140) and the new controller gains .49 and .50. Over the other eight scenarios the difference is −.001, and against the v0.5 detector the new controller is the better one in 58 worlds and the worse in 62. The cadence does the work: cadence alone .8748, rate alone .7732 (below the v0.5 detector), the rate ensemble added to the cadence +.0099. The "learned" rate has two poles written in the code: with near-equal losses the prior mixture gives .369, and once a tenth of recent readings have jumped the weights are replaced by a fixed vector that gives .8273. Every one of the 72 episodes that ends volatile ends at .8273 exactly, with a revisit period of 4 to 6: the controller converges on the fixed-fast setting (.85 and 5) and loses to it by the time it takes to get there (VV at period 8 −.109, at 14 −.063, SV at 14 −.030), winning where the world is stable or slow by re-testing less (SS +.019, VV at 24 +.049). It speeds up and does not slow down again: in the 36 episodes that start volatile and end with more than 100 stable steps, the final rate is .61 to .83 and the final period 6 to 13, against .369 and 29 in a world that was stable throughout. Reading the code, the likely cause is the censoring: readings taken with reserves near capacity are dropped, a stable world fills the reserves, and the window of 54 never flushes. The best of the three fixed settings chosen per scenario scores .8849, the adaptive controller .8847.

**In the theory's terms.** This is E13's tempo result measured by the author on their own world: the knob is how fast a whole gives up what it holds, a slow setting dies in a fast world (.44), a fast one pays a small tax in a stable one (.960 against .979), and an adaptive knob earns back the tax and pays a lag. The adaptive controller is a hazard estimate driving a rate and a clock: the hazard of Experiment 10 in this series, now estimated from readings instead of fixed. That it cannot slow down once its reserves are full is a boundary effect, the ○-as-filter point again: a whole that is doing well stops receiving the readings that would tell it the world has calmed.

## 4g. The integrated agent v0.7 ("Experiment 30"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v07_experiment_30.zip` keeps v0.6 unchanged (the README apart) and adds `circumpunct/strategy_invention.py` and a benchmark: an "attention program" is five fields (failure branch on or off with a threshold of −.028, −.012 or +.008; stale branch on or off with a period of 4, 8 or 16; a recency weight of 0, .55 or 1), read as "if the last reading of the current mode fell below the threshold, or the other mode has not been tried for the period, take the other mode; otherwise take the higher blended estimate". Twelve programs (five hand-written seeds and seven mutations of them) are ranked on 32 training worlds, the top five validated on 40, the winner promoted if it beats the v0.6 controller by more than .01 with a positive lower bound, then scored on 64 fresh worlds and 24 stress worlds against v0.6, two fixed settings and three hand-written rules. The study states that the hand-written failure-only rule matches the promoted program exactly, and that the synthesis adds nothing beyond selecting it. Check script `agent07_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**Numbers.** Both summaries recompute from the 448 and 168 rows to the last digit (means, standard deviations, paired intervals, win counts, by scenario); the study's two tables and paired figures match (holdout: promoted .96087, v0.6 .86103, fixed fast .84727, fixed medium .61837, failure-only .96087, stale-only .85575, recent-only .82437; +.09984 [.07525, .12443], better in 60 of 64; stress: .89253 against .67583, +.21670 [.10470, .32870], 22 of 24); the search and validation tables agree with `learned_program.json`.

**What the study does not say.** The promoted program is candidate 2, the third of the five hand-written seeds in `candidates()`, and it is field for field the "hand-specified failure-only" control: the search returned one of its own starting points, and no mutation produced or improved it. The grammar has one live bit. The seven candidates with a failure branch score .9568 to .9618 on training whatever their threshold, period or recency weight; the five without score .57 to .83. The reason is the world: the right mode moves the reserves by about +.033 a step and the wrong one by about −.048, with noise near .0016, so every threshold in the grammar lies between the two and "switch when the last reading fell" is win-stay, lose-shift, correct one step after any change. The failure branch fires on 5.8% of holdout steps and carries the whole gain. Even with the toggle every 3 steps it scores .70 to .81 where every other controller scores .03 to .39. **In the theory's terms.** Three releases went looking for the right tempo in this world (a map of two tempos, a rate ensemble, a clocked cadence) and the answer is that the world has none to find: one reading tells the truth, so the best gain is 1 and nothing is worth holding. That is E13's law at its limit, persistence pays at the tempo the gain sets and here pays nothing, and it is why a centre of any type could not show itself in v0.5 to v0.7. The rule that wins re-tests on evidence rather than on a clock, which is the one real difference from every controller before it. The author's proposed next step, worlds in which a quick failure response is sometimes harmful, is the right one: lying or blank readings are exactly the conditions under which E12 and E13 found that holding, gating and a centre begin to matter.

## 4h. The integrated agent v0.8 ("Experiment 31"), 2026-11-08

`circumpunct_agent_v08_experiment_31.zip` keeps v0.7 unchanged (README and one capability note apart) and adds `circumpunct/strategy_transfer.py` and a benchmark. **What the study does not say.** This is the first of the agents in which the adaptive layer clearly beats the best single fixed rule (.931 against .831), and the reason is the world: the two fixed rules are each right in one regime and lose a quarter of the score in the other (patient over fast +.226 in the stable class, fast over patient +.258 in the volatile class). The lying readings exist only in the stable state (the shock and the switching cost are both applied only when the hidden state is S), so the benchmark never asks for patience and quickness at once, and an isolated bad reading is itself a mark of the stable state. "Fixed volatile" and "v0.7" are the same rows (the fast program delegates to the inherited decision). The "hand-designed reactive selector" is not an independent control: its code takes program 0 when the context reads volatile and program 2 when it reads stable, indices that fit the order of the source list (fast, verify, patient, probe), but the benchmark passes the saved bank, whose order is patient, fast, verify, probe. It therefore runs the patient program when it believes the world is volatile and verify-twice when it believes it is stable, and its scores sit where that mixture would (volatile class .527 between patient .677 and verify .137; stress volatile class .088, equal to patient). With the intended indices it would be the frozen contextual selector itself. The online update is slightly worse than frozen on the holdout (−.0014 [−.0026, −.0002], identical in 34 of 48 worlds). The stress failure has a plain arithmetic: the ladder calls the world volatile after two runs of two bad readings within 35 steps; at a shock rate of .13 two shocks in a row come about 4 times in 260 steps, at .24 about 15 times. The selector is less patient than the program it is supposed to protect (two readings against three), and in that class it is worse than fixed patient in 11 of 12 worlds. In the stress volatile-to-stable class it scores .605 where both fixed rules score about .22.

**In the theory's terms.** The author took the step named at the end of 4g, and the result is E13's law measured on their own world with both signs: holding through a bad reading is worth .23 where readings sometimes lie and costs .26 where they tell the truth and the world moves. Patience is the hold; the lie here is brief and the truth sustained, so a count of readings in a row separates them, which is a gate on duration. What the benchmark still lacks is the E13 condition proper, lies while the world is changing, since here a lie can only occur when nothing is changing. The stress failure is the same law one level up: a selector that gives up its reading of the context after two bad readings is a fast whole set over a slow one, and it inherits the fast one's distractibility. The context is again two settings of one knob, chosen by a hand-written ladder between two hand-written programs; nothing in it is a centre of any type, and nothing yet requires one.

## 6. A second package: Persistent Center AI v0.1 to v0.3, 2026-11-09

`Persistent_Circumpunct_AI_v0_3.zip` (43 files, dated 2026-11-09) is a different lineage from the agent series: a local dashboard whose "center" is a (goal, interpretive mode) pair kept in a JSON file between runs, plus three synthetic benchmarks shipped with their rows. The mode names (explore, verify, synthesize, remember) are prompt text for an optional local language model that the README says was not tested. Check script `persistent03_check.py` (session scratchpad), no rerun.

**v0.1** (`results/`): a drifting signal with blackouts (observations on 40% of steps), tracked by five predictors over 60 seeds. The summary recomputes. The "center" and the plain alpha-beta filter are the same filter (largest RMSE difference over 60 seeds 4.5e-6, the center's rounding to four decimals): the goals, focus and uncertainty it carries play no part in the prediction. The "reactive" baseline answers zero during every blackout (RMSE 24.5 against last-seen 1.60), a straw man; the real comparison is three textbook filters, and alpha-beta wins (.906 against last-seen 1.60 and EMA 2.56).

**v0.2** (`results/learned/`): an epsilon-greedy contextual bandit over four goals paying .85 against .25, 40 seeds, 650 steps. The summary recomputes. It reaches .78 to .80 of an .85 oracle, does not lock onto one goal when none is better (dominant share .41 against random's .27 in the equal-arms scenario), and without a context label scores .53 where the context decides the best goal. A standard bandit at its own ceiling.

**v0.3** (`results/dual/`): four sources, four bit decoders (as given, inverted, XOR with the current cue, XOR with the previous cue), four contexts, one shift of every rule at the midpoint, 30 seeds of 6000 steps, nine policies. The summary recomputes to the last digit and the README's table, the two paired intervals (joint minus independent +7.07 [+6.25, +7.89], 30 of 30; joint minus no-memory +6.23 [+5.73, +6.73]) and the memory figures (.7775 against .5019) all match.

**What the README does not say.** The joint learner is a 16-arm bandit per context and the "independent" learner averages a source's value over the decoders tried with it, so a rule that is right only as a pair cannot appear in its decision score; the task was built so that only the pair works, and the REPORT itself calls the joint win expected. The memory ablation removes the only correct decoder from a quarter of the contexts, and a quarter of the gap in those trials (.069) is the whole paired gap (.062): the "causal contribution of prior state" is that quarter of the task made unsolvable. With exploration at .16 the greedy ceiling is about .838 and the joint learner reaches .781, 83% of the way from chance; the right pair is 84% of its greedy picks; it scores better after the switch (.802) than over its first half (.781) because the first half includes learning from scratch. The "memory" decoder is a one-step buffer of the public cue; nothing is held longer than one step and nothing through a blank. The center is an argmax over a saved table.

**In the theory's terms.** Persistence here means a file survives a restart, and selection means an argmax; neither is a hold, a rhythm or a centre of any type, and the v0.1 result shows the center's own variables doing nothing. The one structural point the package makes, that focus and interpretation must be learned as a pair when the world rewards only the pair, is the interaction term of a bandit, not a property of the five stations. Nothing here bears on the Theory of Wholeness, and nothing in it is wrong; the authors' own caveats are accurate.
