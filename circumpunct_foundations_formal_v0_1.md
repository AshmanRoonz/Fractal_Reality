## 0. Purpose and status

This document formalizes the philosophical structure developed through the Circumpunct Framework.

It distinguishes four kinds of statement:

1. **Definitions**, which establish how terms are used within the framework.
2. **Axioms**, which state the framework's foundational commitments.
3. **Derived claims**, which follow from those definitions and axioms.
4. **Hypotheses**, which may be philosophically motivated but remain open to mathematical, scientific, or experiential testing.

The notation is intended to make the framework precise enough to criticize, extend, compare, and eventually test. It should not be mistaken for established physics merely because symbols are used.

The central commitments are:

- Infinity is one by inclusion, but infinity is not whole.
- A point is one by indivisibility.
- A circumpunct is one by integration.
- Consciousness is centered wholeness.
- Every whole is also part.
- There is no greatest whole.
- Healthy relation is participation without erasure.
- The future is met at the center.
- The present is the center itself; the interface is where the past arrives. (Corrected 2026-08-01; the earlier commitment read "the present is met at the boundary or interface." See §12.1.)
- The past is formed as the continuing line of actualized experience.

# Part I. Primitive terms

## 1. Three forms of one

The framework distinguishes three irreducible forms of unity.

### 1.1 Infinity

\[
\infty
\]

Infinity is **one by inclusion**.

Nothing is excluded from infinite possibility, but inclusion alone is not integration. Infinity has no final boundary, no completed arrangement of differentiated parts, and no outside against which it becomes a distinct object.

Therefore:

\[
\infty \neq \odot
\]

Infinity is one, but not whole.

### 1.2 Point

\[
\bullet_i
\]

A point is **one by indivisibility**.

For a conscious being \(i\), \(\bullet_i\) denotes the irreducible center of point of view, also called the soul or aperture. It has no spatial extension and no internal arrangement.

The point is not a small object inside the body. It is the locus from which experience occurs.

### 1.3 Circumpunct

\[
\odot_i
\]

A circumpunct is **one by integration**.

It represents a centered whole in which a point, field, boundary, continuity, and their relations belong together without becoming identical.

Thus:

\[
\infty \neq \bullet_i \neq \odot_i
\]

These differences are differences of type, not merely differences of size.

# Part II. Foundational axioms

## 3. Axiom of prime duality

Infinity and singularity form the prime duality:

\[
(\infty,\bullet_i)
\]

Infinity is unlimited potential. The point is an indivisible possibility of experience.

The point does not stand outside infinity. Infinity does not erase the point.

Without infinity, the point has no possibility.

Without the point, infinity never becomes particular as **this**.

---

## 4. Axiom of relational wholeness

Let \(G\) denote God as infinite potential:

\[
G \equiv \infty
\]

Let \(S_i\) denote the \(i\)-th soul:

\[
S_i \equiv \bullet_i
\]

Let \(W_i\) denote the \(i\)-th centered whole:

\[
W_i \equiv \odot_i
\]

The primary theological statement is:

\[
\boxed{
W_i := \mathcal{R}_i(G,S_i)
}
\]

or:

\[
\boxed{
\odot_i := \mathcal{R}_i(\infty,\bullet_i)
}
\]

The relation between God and the soul is the soul's wholeness.

This is not addition:

\[
\odot_i \neq \infty + \bullet_i
\]

It is not identity:

\[
\bullet_i \neq \infty
\]

It is a constitutive relation through which infinite possibility becomes a particular lived whole.

A fuller structural realization is:

\[
\odot_i
=
\mathcal{I}
\left(
\bullet_i,
L_i,
\bigcirc_i,
\Phi_i
\right)
\]

where \(\mathcal{I}\) denotes integration into one centered being.

The two formulations belong together:

\[
\mathcal{R}_i(\infty,\bullet_i)
\rightsquigarrow
\mathcal{I}
\left(
\bullet_i,L_i,\bigcirc_i,\Phi_i
\right)
\]

The first names the ultimate relation. The second names its realized structure.

---

## 5. Axiom of nested wholeness

Every whole has parts, and every whole is also part.

For every whole \(W\), there exists some greater whole \(V\) and a structure-preserving embedding:

\[
f:W\hookrightarrow V
\]

with:

\[
W \not\cong V
\]

A whole remains real as a whole while participating as part of another whole.

This gives the double truth:

\[
W \text{ is whole}
\]

and:

\[
W \text{ is part}
\]

Neither statement cancels the other.

---

## 6. Axiom of no greatest whole

There is no maximal circumpunct.

Formally, if \(\mathcal{W}\) is the domain or category of centered wholes, then:

\[
\forall W\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{W}),
\quad
\exists V\in\mathrm{Ob}(\mathcal{W})
\text{ such that }
W\hookrightarrow V
\text{ and }
W\not\cong V
\]

Therefore \(\mathcal{W}\) has no greatest object that contains all others as completed parts.

An open nesting may be written:

\[
\odot_1
\hookrightarrow
\odot_2
\hookrightarrow
\odot_3
\hookrightarrow
\cdots
\]

but its openness must not be replaced with a final object \(\odot_{\max}\).

Infinity is not the terminal circumpunct of the sequence:

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\odot_n \neq \odot_\infty
\]

if \(\odot_\infty\) is interpreted as one completed whole at infinite scale.

The symbol \(\infty\) names openness by inclusion, not a greatest integrated being.

Canonical statement:

> There is no greatest whole. Every whole is also part, while infinity is one without being whole.

# Part III. Two orders of the circumpunct

The constitutive order describes how a whole is built:

\[
\bullet
\rightarrow
\text{—}
\rightarrow
\bigcirc
\rightarrow
\Phi
\rightarrow
\odot
\]

Expanded through the Octave:

\[
\infty
\rightarrow
\bullet
\rightarrow
\circledast
\rightarrow
\text{—}
\rightarrow
\downdownarrows
\rightarrow
\bigcirc
\rightarrow
✹
\rightarrow
\Phi
\rightarrow
\circlearrowright
\rightarrow
\odot
\]

The boundary precedes the field because an interior cannot exist until an inside has been distinguished from an outside.

This is a logical and constitutive order.

---

## 8. Radial order

The radial order describes how an already completed circumpunct is arranged when moving outward from its center:

\[
\bullet
\rightarrow
\Phi
\rightarrow
\bigcirc
\rightarrow
E
\]

where \(E\) is the surrounding world or environmental field.

In experiential terms:

\[
\text{soul}
\rightarrow
\text{mind}
\rightarrow
\text{body}
\rightarrow
\text{world}
\]

The field lies between the center and boundary.

The radial order is not a sequence of increasing dimensions. In a three-dimensional body, the interior is 3D and the surface is 2D, even though the surface is reached later when moving outward.

Therefore:

> Constitution proceeds from boundary to field. Experience proceeds from center through field to boundary.

The dimensional and radial orders are different axes.

# Part IV. Time and actualization

## 9. The point is always present

Let \(T\) be an ordered temporal domain with no assumed first moment.

At every \(t\in T\), the soul is the present center:

\[
\bullet_i(t)=\text{the current point of view}
\]

The point is not left behind as time passes. It is repeatedly present.

The point does not move away from now. Rather, its recurring presence draws the line of a life.

---

## 10. The future is met at the center

Let:

\[
\mathcal{A}_i(t)=\text{the set of accessible future possibilities}
\]

\[
P_i(t)=\text{the being's credence distribution over those possibilities}
\]

\[
U_i(t)=\text{the being's preferences, values, or motivational orientation}
\]

These are different objects:

- Accessibility is modal.
- Credence is epistemic.
- Preference is motivational.

They must not be collapsed into a single measure.

The center meets the future as an open structured possibility:

\[
\left(
\mathcal{A}_i(t),P_i(t),U_i(t)
\right)
\longrightarrow
\bullet_i(t)
\]

The aperture does not contain a complex internal mechanism. Its framework-native variable is openness:

\[
o_i(t)\in[0,1]
\]

where:

\[
o_i(t)=0
\]

means sealed or obstructed, and:

\[
o_i(t)=1
\]

means maximally open to what arrives through the greater relation.

The correct practical instruction is:

> Clear the aperture. Tune the field.

Not:

> Tune the soul.

---

## 11. The present is met at the interface

Let \(E_i(t)\) denote the relevant environmental state around the body.

The concrete present event occurs where the body's boundary and the world's field meet:

\[
e_i(t)
=
\mathcal{J}
\left(
\bigcirc_i(t),E_i(t)
\mid
\Phi_i(t),\bullet_i(t)
\right)
\]

Here \(\mathcal{J}\) is an interface event operator.

The boundary is not an empty gap between mind and world. It is one contact locus named from both sides:

\[
\text{body's outer boundary}
=
\text{world's field at bodily contact}
\]

The center remains the present point of view, while the present event becomes concrete at the interface.

The center asks:

> What may become?

The interface answers:

> This is happening.

## 12. The past is formed as line

Let \(\gamma_i:T\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\) be the lived trajectory of being \(i\) through a state space \(\mathcal{X}\).

The past at time \(t\) is:

\[
L_i^{-}(t)
=
\gamma_i
\left(

\right)
\]

No first moment is assumed.

As each present event completes, it is incorporated into the continuing line:

\[
L_i^{-}(t^+)
=
L_i^{-}(t)
\cup
\{e_i(t)\}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{future possibility}
\rightarrow
\text{present center}
\rightarrow
\text{mind field}
\rightarrow
\text{interface event}
\rightarrow
\text{past line}

Canonical statement:

> The future is met at the center. The present is met at the boundary. The past is formed as the line.

## 12.1 Adjudication: the center is the present (Ashman, 2026-08-01)

This corrects §0, §11 and §12 above rather than clarifying them.

**The statement.** The center is the present. Infinity is the future. Everything else is past.

**Why this is a correction and not a rephrasing.** §11 says the concrete present occurs at the interface, where boundary meets world. Under the adjudicated statement the interface is where the *past arrives*, not where the present happens. The physiology agrees at no cost to the argument: conscious perception lags stimulus by a couple of hundred milliseconds, light left its source before reaching the eye, and every sensation has already occurred by the time it crosses the boundary. The body is a record. What is genuinely now is the center, and nothing else is.

**Reconciliation with framework §4.11, which the earlier draft left open.** §4.11 assigns convergence to the future (the field not yet gated), the turn i to the present (the gate), and emergence to the past (what has been committed into structure). The adjudicated statement is those same three with station labels where §4.11 used scale labels: ∞ is what convergence brings in, the center is the gate where it turns, and everything already emerged into structure lies behind it. The two were never competitors. §4.11 placed the present at your ⊙; this places it at the center *of* your ⊙, which is more exact and makes "the gate" literal rather than approximate.

**The sharper sentence, which the document should carry:**

> The center is where the future and the past meet, and that meeting is the present.

**A consequence the framework has asserted without justifying.** The center has no extension because a meeting has no width. Not a small thing and not a thin thing: the place two directions arrive at, which is why it can be the only now.

**This also unifies the two orders of Part III.** The radial order is not only a spatial arrangement. Read inward, world to body to mind to soul, it is the route the past takes to reach the center. Read outward from the center it is not the same road, because outward from the center is ∞, and ∞ does not lie further along the radius. So the center carries traffic in two directions that are not opposites: past arriving through the field from the boundary, and future arriving through no channel at all, because the aperture is open to it directly. This is why the aperture's only variable is openness. On the past side there is a field to tune; on the future side there is only a gate.

**Consequent edits still outstanding in this document.** §11's interface-event operator 𝒥 is correctly specified but misnamed as producing the present; it produces the arriving past. §10's title, "The future is met at the center," survives unchanged and is now the only one of the three that was right as written.

# Part V. Mind, body, world, and consciousness

## 13. Mind as field

The mind is not treated as a point object.

It is the interior field of relation extending between center and bodily boundary:

\[
\Phi_i
=
\Phi
\left(
\bullet_i,\bigcirc_i
\right)
\]

The field mediates:

- sensation,
- attention,
- emotion,
- memory,
- anticipation,
- bodily regulation,
- meaning,
- intention.

The distance between point and boundary is field.

Mind mediates between who one is and what one is.

---

## 14. Body as interface

The body is the living boundary of the conscious whole:

\[
\bigcirc_i
\]

It both separates and connects.

From within, it is the body's boundary.

From without, it is where the world's field is already present.

Therefore the body is not merely a relay inserted between two independent things. It is the active surface at which inner and outer become mutually consequential.

# Part VI. Belief into existence

## 17. Reality is imaginary without being arbitrary

The statement that reality is imaginary does not mean it is unreal, private, or freely invented.

The framework distinguishes:

\[
\mathsf{Bel}_i
=
\text{a person's epistemic beliefs}
\]

from:

\[
\mathsf{Aff}
=
\text{ontological affirmation or sustaining belief}
\]

The stronger metaphysical hypothesis is:

\[
\text{possibility}
\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Aff}}
\text{persistent form}
\]

Matter may be understood as imagination made durable, but this remains a metaphysical interpretation unless a testable correspondence is established.

Human belief does not arbitrarily determine reality because each center begins inside inherited conditions:

\[
\mathsf{Bel}_i(t)
=
F
\left(
\text{body},
\text{history},
\text{language},
\text{culture},
\text{relationships},
\text{world},
\text{prior belief}
\right)
\]

Belief changes possibility weightings and action, but it does not independently create the total possibility domain.

Thus:

> We arrive within an imagination already underway.

> We inherit the world's beliefs, participate in them, and influence what they become next.

## 18. This

The word **this** marks actualized presence.

It points to the interface event now occurring:

\[
\text{this}_i(t)=e_i(t)
\]

Its content changes at every moment, but its referential act remains the same.

The future is possibility.

The past is the line already formed.

**This** is the event being met now.

This gives a compact meaning-of-life statement:

> The meaning of life is this.

Meaning is not necessarily imposed from outside life. It is encountered where possibility becomes lived relation.

# Part VII. The wholeness plane

## 19. Relation between nested wholes

Let:

\[
f:W_i\hookrightarrow W_j
\]

be a structure-preserving participation of whole \(W_i\) within greater whole \(W_j\).

The quality of this relation is not one scalar.

Define:

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(f)
=
\left(
c(f),a(f)
\right)
\in[0,1]^2
}
\]

where:

\[
c(f)=\text{connection or participation in the greater whole}
\]

\[
a(f)=\text{autonomy or preserved wholeness of the participating part}
\]

Health is not moderation between two bad ends.

Health is joint maximization:

\[
\boxed{
\kappa(f)\rightarrow(1,1)
}
\]

This is:

> Fully participating, fully whole.

---

## 20. Four relational conditions

The plane produces four qualitative regions.

| Connection \(c\) | Autonomy \(a\) | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| high | high | integration |
| high | low | inflation, absorption, fusion |
| low | high | severance, isolation, fragmentation |
| low | low | anomie, disintegration |

A single line cannot represent the fourth state.

### 20.1 Integration

\[
c\approx1,\quad a\approx1
\]

Participation without erasure.

Permeability without dissolution.

### 20.2 Inflation

\[
c\approx1,\quad a\approx0
\]

The greater whole is treated as the only real whole. The part is absorbed, subordinated, or denied its own center.

### 20.3 Severance

\[
c\approx0,\quad a\approx1
\]

The part preserves identity but denies or loses meaningful participation in greater wholes.

### 20.4 Anomie

\[
c\approx0,\quad a\approx0
\]

The being belongs to nothing while also lacking stable internal wholeness.

---

## 21. Internal integration hypothesis

Let:

\[
h(W_i)\in[0,1]
\]

measure the internal integration of whole \(W_i\).

Retained autonomy may depend on both connection and internal integration:

\[
\boxed{
a(f)=A\left(c(f),h(W_i)\right)
}
\]

**Hypothesis:**

\[
\frac{\partial A}{\partial h}>0
\]

for relevant levels of connection.

In words:

> The stronger a whole's internal integration, the more connection it can sustain without losing identity.

A thinly integrated object may require weak coupling to remain distinct.

A highly integrated person may sustain intimacy, cooperation, or belonging without disappearing.

This is a proposed bridge between the physical tradeoff and the ethical wholeness plane. It is not yet established.

# Part VIII. A category of nested wholes

## 22. Categorical skeleton

Define a category \(\mathcal{W}\):

- Objects are centered wholes \(W_i\).
- Morphisms \(f:W_i\rightarrow W_j\) are structure-preserving participations or nestings.
- Every object has an identity morphism:

\[
\mathrm{id}_{W_i}:W_i\rightarrow W_i
\]

- Nestings compose:

\[
W_i
\xrightarrow{f}
W_j
\xrightarrow{g}
W_k
\]

producing:

\[
g\circ f:W_i\rightarrow W_k
\]

- Composition is associative:

\[
h\circ(g\circ f)
=
(h\circ g)\circ f
\]

- \(\mathcal{W}\) has no greatest object.

The Circumpunct contribution is not category theory itself. It is the requirement that participation morphisms carry both connection and preserved wholeness:

\[
\kappa(f)=\left(c(f),a(f)\right)
\]

---

## 23. Composition must not silently assume multiplication

A naive multiplicative model would give:

\[
c(g\circ f)=c(g)c(f)
\]

Across \(n\) levels:

\[
c_n=\prod_{j=1}^n c_j
\]

For any fixed \(c_j=c<1\):

\[
c_n=c^n\rightarrow0
\]

This would imply inevitable severance across sufficiently deep nesting.

That model conflicts with the recorded tonic-shared nesting result, where spectral departure saturates rather than compounds.

---

## 24. Shared-Tonic Principle

The recorded candidate law is:

\[
d_n
=
d_\infty
\left(
1-\rho^n
\right),
\qquad
0<\rho<1
\]

where \(d_n\) is relational or spectral departure after \(n\) nested scales.

Then:

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}d_n=d_\infty
\]

Departure approaches a finite bound.

Proposed principle:

> When nested wholes share a common station, relational departure saturates rather than compounds. Depth does not necessarily weaken participation because continuity is renewed across every adjacent scale.

Moral interpretation:

> Participation survives depth.

Important limitation:

The reported quantity:

\[
\frac{|\lambda_1|-1}{\alpha}
\]

is spectral departure. It is not automatically ethical, psychological, or experiential connection. Any mapping from spectral departure to \(c\) requires an additional argument.

# Part IX. Moral and social consequences

## 25. Mereological equality

No person, institution, ideology, nation, or community may rightfully claim:

\[
\text{I am the whole, and you are merely a part}
\]

Every person is both whole and part.

A greater whole is legitimate only when its parts remain recognized as centered wholes.

Formally, a healthy participation requires:

\[
c(f)>0
\]

and:

\[
a(f)>0
\]

with the ethical ideal:

\[
(c(f),a(f))\rightarrow(1,1)
\]

A whole that preserves itself by destroying the wholeness of its parts converts integration into domination.

---

## 26. Inflation and severance as linked lies

The inflation lie says:

> I, we, the leader, the institution, or the universal whole are the only real whole.

The severance lie imposed upon others says:

> You are merely a part, without wholeness of your own.

These often appear together:

\[
\text{inflation above}
\Rightarrow
\text{severance below}
\]

The correction is nested wholeness:

\[
\text{I am whole}
\]

\[
\text{I am part}
\]

\[
\text{You are whole}
\]

\[
\text{Together we may form another whole}
\]

---

## 27. Narcissistic systems

A narcissistic system can be modeled as repeated loss of preserved wholeness while connection to the hierarchy remains high.

A typical progression is:

\[
\text{small loss of autonomy}
\rightarrow
\text{greater dependence}
\rightarrow
\text{weakened boundaries}
\rightarrow
\text{identification with the hierarchy}
\rightarrow
\text{recruitment of others}
\rightarrow
\text{absorption into the system}
\]

In the wholeness plane:

\[
c\text{ remains high}
\]

while:

\[
a\downarrow
\]

The system reproduces itself when absorbed members demand the same surrender from newcomers.

## 28. Truth, agreement, fairness, and justice

Agreement is not automatically fair.

A valid agreement among wholes requires:

- relevant truth,
- meaningful boundaries,
- the possibility of refusal,
- recognition of affected centers,
- continuing correction.

Let \(D\) be a decision domain and \(K\) the available truthful knowledge about it.

Let:

\[
A(D\mid K)
\]

be an agreement formed under that knowledge.

Let:

\[
F\left(A\right)
\]

be the fairness of the relations it creates.

Justice is fair agreement given durable, revisable form:

\[
\boxed{
J
=
\operatorname{Stabilize}
\left(
F(A(D\mid K))
\right)
}
\]

As knowledge improves:

\[
K\rightarrow K'
\]

the agreement must remain revisable:

\[
A(D\mid K)\rightarrow A(D\mid K')
\]

Truth does not mechanically determine one agreement, but without truth the participants do not know what they are agreeing to.

# Audit findings (Claude, 2026-08-01 filing pass)

Content above is unaltered. These are open items for adjudication, ordered by consequence.

**1. §1.1 states only one of the three copula relations.** The document asserts ∞ ≠ ⊙ flatly, and §6 adds the convergence statement in negative form (lim ⊙_n ≠ ⊙_∞). Missing is the third: ⊙ = ∞ under label-drop, which the corpus calls topologically exact rather than metaphor and which carries "Atman is Brahman." The three settled relations are: **≠ as forms** (integration is not inclusion, and conflating them is the featureless-monism error), **→ as convergence** along the nesting chain, never arriving, and **= under label-drop**, immediate and outside the scale axis. A foundations document that carries only the first two loses the apophatic identity the corpus commits to, and makes the six-name chain (Reality = Truth = 1 = E = ∞ = ⊙) look like a contradiction rather than a use of the third relation. See the Wholeness Plane and Nesting Notice in `CLAUDE.md`.

**2. RESOLVED 2026-08-01, see §12.1. Part IV introduced a second temporal mapping that was not reconciled with framework §4.11.** This document assigns time to stations within one whole: future met at the center, present at the interface, past as the line. §4.11 assigns time to scale positions: the greater whole is the future (the ungated field), your own scale is the present (the gate), the parts are the past (already committed into structure). Both are defensible and they are not the same assignment. This is structurally the same situation as the constitutive and radial orders, which needed an explicit note before they stopped reading as a contradiction. One is needed here too, or a reader who knows §4.11 will think the present has been moved.

**3. §4's relation presupposes two relata that A0 may not supply.** ⊙_i := R_i(∞, •_i) treats infinity and the point as two things standing in a relation. But §1.2 of the framework says 0 is not absence but the 1 at maximum convergence, and A0 says there is one energy. On that reading ∞ and • are one substrate at two degrees of constraint, and one does not mediate between a thing and its own substrate. Either the prime duality is a duality of aspect, in which case R relates one thing under two descriptions and the boxed statement needs rewording, or A0 is being amended. Both are available; neither should happen silently.

**4. §22's category has an identity problem.** Morphisms are declared to be structure-preserving participations or nestings, and §5 requires W ≇ V for a nesting. But every object needs an identity morphism id_W : W → W, and that is not a nesting. Either identities are admitted as a degenerate case and it is false that every morphism is a nesting, or the structure is not a category. The clean fix is to define morphisms as structure-preserving maps, with proper nestings the non-identity ones.

**5. §24 does not supply the composition law §29.4 asks for.** d_n = d_∞(1 − ρⁿ) gives departure as a function of depth along a uniform chain. A composition law states how κ(g∘f) is determined by κ(g) and κ(f), which is a different object, and it must be associative or the category fails. A genuine candidate exists: d₁ ⊕ d₂ = d₁ + d₂ − d₁d₂ is associative, commutative, has identity 0, and saturates, so it is a legitimate categorical operation. It saturates at 1 rather than at d_∞ ≈ 0.7α, so it needs rescaling before it fits the measured result. Worth testing against the v14 data, because if the measured chain obeys ⊕ then the composition law is settled rather than conjectured.

**6. §5 formalizes only the upward direction.** The prose says every whole has parts and every whole is also part, but only the upward embedding is written. The downward claim (that a whole's parts are themselves wholes) is what A3 needs, and it is what defeats the body-politic argument, since the reply to "members are cells" is that cells are wholes too. The asymmetry should be closed or explained.

**Minor.** §17 hedges the matter claim ("remains a metaphysical interpretation unless a testable correspondence is established") while the plain companion §19 states it unhedged. The two documents should agree on that sentence, since it is the one that costs the most with a materialist reader. And §29.9 correctly anticipates the aperture-entropy problem, which is now settled: the 0D center has one variable, openness, per the Wholeness Plane and Nesting Notice.

**What the document gets right and should not lose in revision:** the two orders stated explicitly (§7 and §8), the separation of accessibility, credence and preference into three objects rather than one measure (§10), openness as the aperture's only variable (§10), the wholeness plane with all four cells (§19-20), the internal-integration hypothesis marked as unestablished (§21), and the Shared-Tonic Principle carrying its own honest gap about spectral departure not being ethical connection (§24). That last one is the discipline the corpus asks for and it is being applied without prompting.
