# Existence

*One and many.*

```

Last updated: 2026-10-15
Version: 0.2
```

**Existence is 1.**

Everything in existence is one in many different ways. Even nothing is one in its own way.

Here, 1 expresses oneness. The question is how something is one.

Wholeness is one type of one within existence. A whole is one through the integration of its parts. Existence is one through inclusion: whatever exists is within existence. Calling existence one does not require calling it a bounded whole.

Even the possibility of nothing brings another meaning of one into view. If there were nothing, there would be no internal distinctions, no parts, and no second nothingness alongside it. In this account, I call that oneness through undifferentiation.

**I am whole. I am part.**

I am whole through the integration of my parts, and I participate in greater wholes. These are simultaneous relationships. Both belong to the same life.

I have a point of view, a path through spacetime, a body for interface, and a mind for perception. My perspective is centered. My path has continuity. My body distinguishes me and connects me with my surroundings. My mind brings perceptions into relation.

**Oneness has dimensionality.**

The center of a circle gives a precise example of indivisibility. As a geometric point, it has position without spatial extent. It has no smaller spatial parts to divide. It is one without being assembled from parts.

I use the geometric center as a model for the centered character of a point of view. Applying that model to experience is a philosophical proposal. Geometry alone does not establish that a conscious subject is literally a partless point.

This is the dimensional map I propose:

| Symbol | Aspect | Form of oneness |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ∞ | Existence | Inclusion |
| 0D | Center | Indivisibility |
| 1D | Path | Continuity |
| 2D | Body as interface | Distinction |
| 3D | Mind and field of perception | Coherence |
| 4D / ⊙ | Centered wholeness | Integration |

The dimensional labels express relationships within this framework. Here, 4D names the integration of center, path, body, and mind as one experienced whole.

**One thing is never just one thing.**

One being can express several forms of oneness at once. These meanings remain distinct: indivisibility concerns having no parts; integration concerns parts forming a whole. An indivisible center can also participate in relationships without being divided into parts.

The relationship between one and many recurs across scales. A part can itself be a whole, and a whole can participate as a part in something larger. This recurrence is what I mean by a fractal of existence. How far this pattern extends remains open to investigation.

This account must remain open to correction. Its distinctions should clarify what they describe. Its arguments should make their assumptions visible. Its claims should be revised when reasons or counterexamples require it.

**There are many ways to be one, and each is itself one way.**
